> ## KEEP THIS DOCUMENT CURRENT
> **Every developer (human or Claude) who changes gameplay, numbers, UI flow, economy, raids, controls or editions MUST update this document in the same commit**, then:
> 1. bump the document version below (patch for corrections, minor for new systems),
> 2. add a row to the *Revision history*,
> 3. update `docs/CHANGELOG.md` and the status in `docs/HANDOFF.md`.
>
> Numbers here mirror the code (`Economy`, `SupportRules`, `CannonUpgrades`, `Rules`, `RaidTypes`, `DefenseConfig`); if code and this document disagree, the code is right and this document is a bug. Items marked **(TBD)** are not designed yet; items marked **(unverified)** exist in code but were never playtested.

| 1.1.4 | 2026-10-15 | Development (branch `feature/raid-control`, from 1.1.3) | **New patch, new pain, new feature (owner).** *Crafting cost balancer* (`CraftBalance`, `data/eternal_defense/balance.json`): each gun's damage output (damage a second over a full magazine and its reload, bolt actions waiting for the bolt, a blast added to the shot) is compared with the median of the guns of its Age (an Age with fewer than 3 guns is not judged); from 1.4 times the median a gun needs a rare part, one more every 0.8 above, at most 4: a diamond in Ages 1 and 2, 2 emeralds a part in Age 3, 3 and 4 blaze rods a part in Ages 4 and 5; a recipe whose author already asks for a diamond, emerald or blaze rod is left alone (the Apocalypse pack's No. 3 Revolver, Age 1, 1.47 times its peers, keeps its one diamond). The payout percent raises every stack of the completion cache. Network protocol **42** (the beacon state carries the Raid control numbers, the `raid-mod:` action). |

## 1. Vision

Eternal Defense turns a TaCZ gun-pack world into a **shared base-defense campaign**. Players plant one Defense Beacon, build a base worth defending around it, and survive raids that grow with the base and the team.
Industry (Create) feeds the war effort; farming and a tiered kitchen feed the defenders; every player also owns a personal support gun that answers their call. The fantasy: *you hold the line, the factory keeps you supplied, and the cannon behind you is yours.*

**Pillars**
1. **The base is the objective.** Lose the beacon and the campaign ends; hold it and everything improves.
2. **A reason to build well.** Base quality raises rewards and makes raids bigger, so beauty and function are the same investment.
3. **Progress, not tedium.** A won raid replenishes ammunition and moves the player forward; a bad raid is recoverable.
4. **Two ways to power:** a factory (fast, big) or target farming (slower, hands-on). Both stay practical.
5. **Readable and teachable.** Clear icons, plain requirements, a field guide, and a warning before every raid.

**Target players:** a small co-op team on a modded server or in single player with the Eternal Defense pack (the standalone edition for any TaCZ world was discontinued in 0.0.41). Multiplayer shared campaign; each player owns their own support gear.

## 3. Mods in the package

* **Defense Beacon** (`eternal_defense`): the campaign, raids, support system, Weapon Platform, Exchange.
* **Gun Guide**: client HUD showing the real controls of the held gun, with the player's own key bindings, key-conflict warnings and a jam alert.
* **Gun Displays**: eleven stands, wall racks and glass cases for showing guns. Decoration only; they count like any other placed block for base score.

## 4. Core loop

1. **Prepare** (preparation phase, daytime): build, farm, prepare sandwiches and stock communal stew, craft, trade, upgrade, set the raid level.
2. **Scan:** when the raid starts, the base is scored and snapshotted.
3. **Raid:** waves of attackers come from beyond the zone, breach, dig and shoot; the team defends the beacon.
4. **Resolve:** victory repairs all damage and pays rewards (beacon is the reward chest, plus Ardent Energy); defeat leaves the ruins.
5. **Announce next type:** the kind of the next raid is shown at once.
6. **Spend:** upgrades, flares, Exchange; loop back, stronger.

## Feature guide for designers

**How to read this.** Section 5 is the rulebook with the exact numbers and the code names. This section goes feature by feature and says, in plain words: what the feature is, what the player does with it, how it behaves, what job it does in the game, and which dials change it. It is written for someone who has not read the code. A line marked *Not played yet* has only been checked by unit tests, a real-client run or a code read, never by a team playing several raids (see section 9). Where the pack and standalone editions differ, both are named. If this guide and the code disagree, the code is right.

### The game in one minute

A team plants one **Defense Beacon**. It makes a protected **zone** around it. Inside the zone the team builds; the beacon **scores the base**, and the score sets a **reward tier** from 0 to 10. At night, **raids** march on the beacon, harder and more often at higher tiers. Winning pays loot and **Ardent Energy** (the soft currency) and puts back everything the raiders broke. Energy and materials buy **guns, ammo and gear** at the workshop tables, a personal **support cannon** that delivers supplies, rescues and bombardments, and **beacon upgrades**. A **kitchen** turns food into short combat buffs. Everything is taught by an in-game **Field Guide**. The loop is: prepare, survive, spend, get stronger, face a bigger raid.

### Part A. The campaign

#### Defense Beacon
* **What it is.** The heart of the campaign: a glowing crystal block. One per world, Overworld only, shared by the whole team. It cannot be broken or moved by normal play.
* **What the player does.** Plants it once (a confirmation screen explains what cannot be undone). Right-clicks it to open four tabs: Overview, Upgrades, Workshop Fabrication and Raid break. Settings and the Field Guide sit in the header.
* **How it behaves.** Planting starts the campaign and a short first raid at once. The beacon has health. A bigger Core raises it. Raiders hitting it, and defenders dying near it (every third death; since 0.0.41 a skull with the deaths left sits on its health bar during a raid), lower it. At zero it switches off: that is a defeat. Wild hostile mobs can also hit an exposed beacon they can see from 24 blocks, even during a raid break, so players cover it with solid blocks. During a raid cover no longer helps (0.0.21): every hostile mob within 48 blocks of the zone's edge comes for the beacon. Repairing (Overview, Repair) gives +250 health and restarts the campaign. The cost is 6 Ardent Energy (standalone) or 8 of the pack part.
* **Job in the game.** It gives the fight a place and the team one thing to lose (pillar 1). The planting spot is the biggest permanent decision of the game.
* **Dials.** Health and radius per Core level in `Rules`; repair price in `Economy`.
* **Designer watch-out.** The only exit is the full reset (see Recovery Shovel). New players are warned in the plant confirmation, the beacon tooltip and the Field Guide.

#### Base zone
* **What it is.** The area around the beacon that belongs to the team, drawn as a moving cyan grid (it can be switched off in Settings).
* **What the player does.** Builds inside it. Places the kitchen, Exchange Shop and support gear only inside it.
* **How it behaves.** Radius grows with Core: **8, 12, 18, 24** blocks. The Vertical branch makes it taller: it starts 3 blocks down and 8 up and reaches 16 down and 48 up at the fifth level. The zone is centred on the beacon. A clear 3 × 3 space, two blocks tall, around the beacon is needed for the largest Core level. Weapon tables placed inside count toward the base score.
* **Job in the game.** It is the design boundary: everything the player owns that matters is inside, and the raid spawns are outside. It also defines "home" for meal doubling (see Mess Hall).
* **Dials.** Radii per Core level; vertical limits; the zone grid toggle is a client setting.

#### Base score and reward tier
* **What it is.** A grade for the base. When a raid starts the beacon scans the zone: structure, material palette, detail blocks (stairs, slabs, fences, doors), lighting, furnishings (beds, bookshelves, barrels, chests, tables), usable closed rooms, machinery and weapon stations.
* **What the player sees.** The Overview shows the score and the reward tier, and the score's ten cells (since 0.0.41: Build, Palette, Details, Lighting, Installations, Furnishings, Layout, Factory, Platforms, Total).
* **Installations (0.0.41, owner).** Each of the mod's installations standing in the zone adds 25 points; the whole set of 20 adds 500, exactly one building tier (`Rules.INSTALLATION_SET_SCORE` = `BUILDING_TIER_SCORE`, `BaseScoring.INSTALLATIONS`): the Exchange Shop, the Support Platform and Cannon, the four weapon tables, the Mess Hall, Cook Pot, Bowl and Milk Dispensers, the Drink Station, Brew Pot and Cup Dispenser, the MRE Station, the Logistics Center Hub, the Command Table, the Satellite Beacon, the Supply Receiving Gate and the Tactical Enchanting Table. Each counts once (a Mess Hall or Drink Station of any Mk is one); the Defense Beacon (always there) and the FOB-only blocks (FOB Beacon, Supply Catapult, Supply Delivery Gate) are not in the set. It is counted on top of whatever else a block scores (the weapon tables' Age points, the cannon's machinery points). The Overview's Installations cell reads "100 (4/20)".
* **How it behaves.** The tier runs 0 to 10. Each of the five Logistics levels adds one tier; a good base adds up to five more. A higher tier makes the next raids bigger, makes them come more often (about weekly at tier 0, down to every two days at the top) and makes the rewards bigger. Natural terrain scores nothing, and ore blocks score no more than plain blocks.
* **Job in the game.** It makes building well and playing well the same investment (pillar 2): a beautiful, functional base is both the reward engine and the difficulty setter. The raid level cap is the escape valve for a team that grew faster than its gear.
* **Dials.** Scoring weights in the base-scoring code; tier from Logistics in `Rules`; schedule intervals per tier.
* **Not played yet.** Whether the scoring weights reward the right things, and whether the tier curve feels fair.

#### Beacon upgrades (six branches)
Bought in the Upgrades tab with the team standing near the beacon, between raids. In Creative everything is free. Packing up the beacon wipes every upgrade.

| Branch | What it does | Levels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Wider zone, tougher beacon (radius 8, 12, 18, 24) | 3 upgrades (Mk-1 to Mk-4) |
| Logistics | Each level adds one reward tier | 5 |
| Defense | 15, 30, 45 and 60 percent less damage to the beacon | 4 |
| Restoration | Faster rebuilding after a raid; the beacon heals 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 percent of its health after a victory | 5 |
| Vertical | Zone height: it starts 3 blocks down and 8 up and ends 16 down and 48 up | 5 |
| Reconnaissance | Raiders still alive glow through walls sooner: after 5 minutes of a wave without it, then 3, 1, and from the start of the wave (0.0.18; it was one purchase and the base was one minute) | 3 |

* **Prices.** Standalone, in Ardent Energy: **4, 8, 14, 22, 32** by level. Pack: **8, 16, 32, 64, 128** of the branch's own factory part: Core and Defense reinforced plating (Defense was resonance coils before 0.0.18), Logistics logistics modules, Restoration restoration matrices, Reconnaissance resonance coils; the Vertical zone takes a quarter of that of every one of the four parts (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 each; before 0.0.18 it was logistics modules only). **0.0.28 (owner):** every price in restoration matrices is halved, rounded up (`Economy.matrices`): Restoration 4, 8, 16, 32, 64; the Vertical zone's matrix share 1, 2, 4, 8, 16.
* **Making the parts (pack, KubeJS `15_beacon_recipes.js`).** Reinforced Beacon Plating: since 0.0.41 (owner) compacting in a Basin under a Mechanical Press: 2 iron sheets, 2 golden sheets and 2 copper sheets into **1** plating, with no heat (until 0.0.40: 4 hardened alloy and a brass sheet, heated, for 4). The other parts are unchanged (logistics modules by sequenced assembly, resonance coils by heated mixing, restoration matrices by mechanical crafting, 8 a craft).
* **Job in the game.** The permanent sink for energy and factory output, and the only way to raise the reward tier by spending.
* **Designer watch-out.** Logistics raises both rewards and danger. The Field Guide says so in red on that card.

#### Reward chest
* **What it is.** The beacon itself is the chest. A 54-slot chest for the whole team, opened from the Overview.
* **How it behaves.** Prizes are put in each time the screen opens; whatever does not fit waits in a queue. If the beacon is destroyed, the contents spill out. If the campaign is packed up, unclaimed prizes are thrown away.
* **Pack edition (0.0.28, owner):** every won raid's prizes include exactly one Pressed Receiver, a hard raid too (added after the hard raid's bonus).
* **Job in the game.** Keeps raid loot out of the pack so it cannot be lost on death, and gives raids a clear "collect" moment.

#### Recovery Shovel
* **What it is.** A special iron shovel and the player's remote control for the beacon. Everyone starts with one; it is craftable (iron above and below, copper left and right, redstone in the middle).
* **What the player does.** Right-clicks the air to open the beacon panel from far away; hits a monster to knock it back (it deals no damage, whatever the player's strength); uses it on their own Support Platform or Cannon to pick it up free of charge, keeping upgrades and contents; uses it on the beacon to pack the whole campaign up.
* **How it behaves.** Using it on the beacon asks for confirmation. The result is a full reset: every upgrade, win and reward tier returns to zero, raids stop, unclaimed prizes are thrown away, the player gets one fresh beacon back and the buildings stay.
* **Job in the game.** The only way to relocate or abandon a base. It makes the plant decision weighty without making it a trap.
* **Designer watch-out.** Anyone with a shovel can reset the world for everyone. The game asks first; there is no permission system.

### Part B. Raids

#### Raid schedule and control
* **What it is.** The pace of the game: when raids come and how hard they are allowed to be.
* **How it behaves.** An introduction raid starts the moment the beacon is planted: short, gentle, always ordinary. Winning it hands over the first Weapon Platform. After that raids come only at night (0.0.21: every raid but the introduction), every 10 active days at reward tier 0, one day sooner per tier, down to every 3 days from tier 7. The clock counts **active days** only (days when someone is online). A raid that is due waits for the night and for a defender near the beacon.
* **What the player controls (Raid control tab, 1.1.4; it was the Raid break tab).** Two pages. *Level & break* shows two cards side by side: the cyan level card (the cap with - and +, "Next raid: level N", how it is made: base + break bonus within the cap, then the waves, mobs a wave, elites and Ardent pay of that raid) and the brass break card (days to the next raid, days bought and the level bonus, the next price). Start raid (red since 1.1.5, like every button that starts a raid) sits under the level card, Buy five active days under the break card. *Raid modifiers* holds the special raid row and the modifiers (below). With little room (GUI scale 3 at 720p) the numbers are plain lines; with more they are chips and each card adds a note.
  * *Raid level cap*, a dial from 0 to 10. Lower it for an easier raid and a smaller prize; it never touches base score or upgrades. The whole team shares it.
  * *Start raid now.* Starts a raid at the shown level at a time of the team's choosing. The base is scanned first and any bought break ends. It cannot be undone. By day (0.0.21) it books the raid for nightfall instead: the button reads "Raid at nightfall" and greys out, and the raid starts at dusk if someone is near the beacon.
  * *Raid break.* Pay to push the next raid back five active days. Each purchase costs more than the last and purchases add up. Every break also makes the next raid tougher and richer (up to three tiers, never above the cap). Pack price: brass, precision mechanisms and gold. Standalone: copper, diamonds and gold.
* **Job in the game.** Three ways to trade risk against reward without ever stalling the game: wait, lower, or push on. The break is a deliberate "pay for time, get a bigger raid" trade.
* **Dials.** Intervals per tier (`Rules.intervalDays`), the raid hours (`Rules.RAID_HOUR_FROM`, `RAID_HOUR_UNTIL`) and break prices in `Rules` and `Economy`.

#### Anatomy of a raid
* **What happens.** The base is scored and snapshotted. Waves then come from outside the zone: **3 + tier** waves, with at most five extra. Raiders scale with the number of defenders. They spawn on open surface ground at or above the beacon's level, never underground (the rule relaxes after 30 and 90 seconds if no such spot exists), walk **straight at the beacon**, and only stop to dig through doors and walls when they stop making progress. Each raider wears a **red exclamation mark** and is immune to sunlight. Ranged raiders hold their distance. Any raider left alive after one minute glows.
* **A raider that cannot arrive never decides the raid.** Raiders are not given detours (they march straight, by design), so the game watches for two things. If the straight line ahead (10 blocks) shows lava or magma, a drop of 6 or more blocks or 8 or more blocks of water in a row (a short pond is waded), or if a raider has not got 3 blocks closer to the beacon for 25 seconds while it is not fighting, shooting or digging, it **opens a red gate**: it stops and channels for 8 seconds beside the gate, which anybody can see, and then steps out of a second red gate at the edge of the staging ring (it opens 3 seconds before, so players near the exit see where the raider will come out, and closes 2 seconds after), away from every player, and marches again. While it channels it can be shot: each second it is hurt delays it by 3 seconds (never more than 10 in all), and killing it cancels the gate. Raiders stuck at the same place share one gate (8 channel at a time, the rest wait). After three gates a raider is withdrawn (no drop; chat says "N stuck raiders withdrew" once per wave); a raid boss is gated again each time and never withdrawn. Flyers, parachutists in the air and Special Forces soldiers are left alone. The spawn directions are also checked once per raid for water, lava and cliffs, and clean directions are used first.
* **How it ends.** Victory: everything the raid broke is put back (a damage journal), and the prizes go to the reward chest, with Ardent Energy and Ammo Coins. Defeat: the beacon switches off and the damage stays until repaired. Spent ammo, fuel and tool wear are never refunded either way.
* **Job in the game.** The pressure the whole game pushes against. Straight-line marching makes the fight readable and the base's geometry matter.
* **Dials.** Wave count, spawn rules and attacker counts in the raid code (`Rules`, `RaidTypes`); stall time, progress distance, the probe limits, channel time, damage delay, gate count and capacity and the busy rules in `RaidMarch`; corridor rules in `RaidSpawns`.
* **Not played yet.** Ground spawning for Air, Siege and Swarm raids in a real world; two-human co-op; whether 25 seconds, 3 blocks, 8 seconds of channel and 3 gates feel fair on real terrain, and how the red gate reads in play (the diagnostics log shows what a stuck raider stood on).

#### Boss raids
* **What it is.** Every third raid is a boss raid. It is simply a stronger raid. It has nothing to do with how often raids come.
* **How it behaves.** 25 percent more attackers, a boss arrives with the final wave, completion rewards are 50 percent larger. In the pack the boss is a modded boss with a red boss bar, and beating it also gives bonus medical kits and grenades; in standalone, without boss mods, it is a vanilla Ravager. The Overview announces when the next raid is a boss raid.
* **Job in the game.** A rhythm: two raids to recover and prepare, a third that tests the build.

#### Special raids
* **What it is.** A raid with a twist, each asking for a different counter-build. Since 1.1.4 (owner) the team **chooses** it on the Raid modifiers page (one at a time, or Ordinary); no raid is rolled any more. Choosing one tells every player ("Next raid: AIR RAID"); the Overview shows it.

| Type | What it asks of the base |
| --- | --- |
| Air raid | Flying mobs ignore walls. Build anti-air, not thicker walls. |
| Paratroopers | Heavy mobs drop from high up on red parachutes; shoot them while they float. |
| Siege | Creepers dig into walls and shooters fire through the holes. |
| They are thousands | Only zombies and husks, double count, spawning faster, up to 96 alive. |

* **Reward.** A won special raid adds ceil((3 + 2 × tier) × 1.25) Ardent Energy (4 at level 0, 17 at 5, 29 at 10), and its mobs drop Ardent 15% more often.
* **Job in the game.** Variety on demand. Each type punishes one defensive habit (wall-only bases, one weapon type, no area damage).
* **Dials.** `RaidTypes`: count factors, `bonusEnergy`; `RaidModifiers.SPECIAL_DROPS`.
* **Not played yet.** Does the air raid need a stronger anti-air toolkit than the player has?

#### Elite mobs (1.1.4)
* **What it is.** A raid mob with one power, named over its head in gold (red from Badass IV). Since 1.1.8 a ring turns at its feet and a health bar floats over its head, both full bright in its kind's colour, so it stands out at night. Owner's idea: elites start at raid level 0 with at least one in the whole raid, reach one every wave at level 5 and several a wave at level 10.
* **How it behaves.** Elites a wave on average: level / 5 below level 5 (one wave of each raid, picked at the start, always has one), 1 at level 5, then 0.4 more a level (3 at level 10); Elite Cadre adds one a wave. The wave's elites are spread over its spawns. Never in the introduction raid, never a boss or a Special Forces soldier. The first elite of a wave is announced in chat ("Wave N: an elite is here, Badass III Zombie! This wave pays 1 more Ardent Energy."); every wave that had one adds 1 Ardent Energy to the completion reward. Every elite drops its own Ardent Energy when it dies (its Badass level, 2 for Healer, Matrioska and Long Shot, 1 for the rest), on top of the usual raid roll.

| Kind (weight) | Power |

From Badass II a giant: stuck in a raid, it smashes the blocks its body runs into (up to 24 a second, from its feet up; `RaidBreaching.smash`). * **Job in the game.** A named threat in every raid that asks for attention (focus the Healer, keep the Matrioska away from the wall, take the Long Shot first) and pays for it.
* **How it looks (1.1.8).** Colours (`Elites.Kind.color`): Badass gold (red from IV), Speedster yellow, Healer green, Matrioska pink, Long Shot sky blue, Flak Armor silver, Body Armor bronze, Vampiric crimson, Frostbite ice blue, Berserker orange. Any mob can be an elite, modded ones too; a Badass is drawn at its size whatever renders it (vanilla, GeckoLib, entity model packs); its name, health bar and mark float above its hitbox by their usual gap times its size (a grown model reaches higher above its hitbox too: Born in Chaos' Door Knight raises its axe two blocks over it at five times).
* **Dials.** `Elites` (weights, numbers, Badass odds and size, colours), `Elites.perWave`.
* **Not played yet.** Whether Long Shot arrows, Matrioska blasts and Badass V at high levels are fair; whether five-times giants smashing walls feel fair or wreck too much of a base.

#### Raid modifiers (1.1.4; drops 1.1.5)
* **What it is.** Harder raids by choice, on the Raid modifiers page of Raid control, for the next raid only. They pay more: each raises the completion cache by a percent and the Ardent drop chance of raid mobs by a percent of the base chance. Chosen near the beacon between raids, after the introduction raid; used up when the raid starts, whether someone presses Start raid or the raid timer runs out (chat names them); the choice resets after the raid. With all six and a special raid a marked mob drops at twice the base chance (1.5 without any).

| Modifier | Effect | Payout | Ardent drops |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reinforcements | 30% more mobs every wave | +20% | +8% |
| Elite Cadre | one more elite every wave | +15% | +9% |
| Hardened | raid mobs +35% health | +15% | +8% |
| Forced March | raid mobs +20% speed | +10% | +6% |
| Fog of War | switches off the Reconnaissance upgrade for that raid: no raid mob glows | +10% | +5% |
| Exposed Beacon | mobs that hit the beacon do 50% more | +15% | +6% |
| A special raid (one at a time) | its own twist (below) | its own Ardent bonus | +8% |

* **Job in the game.** Owner: "player can choose to activate debuff next raid to increase the raid payout and get a % bonus ardent drop chance". A team that is ahead can farm more, a team that is behind is never forced.
* **Dials.** `RaidModifiers.MODS` (id, payout, drops) and its effect constants.

#### Special Forces
* **What it is.** Armed soldiers who join raids once the reward tier is 4 or more.
* **How it behaves.** A warning comes first. One soldier per wave at tiers 4 to 6, heavy soldiers from tier 7. They shoot from far away, so cover (sandbags) matters. Their bodies can be looted for about ten minutes.
* **Job in the game.** An answer to teams that ignore ranged play, and a loot source for the high tiers.

#### Trap learning
* **What it is.** Raiders get used to the same kind of trap.
* **How it behaves.** The first wave takes full damage from a trap class. Each later wave takes 25 percent less from it, until after four steps it does nothing. Eleven classes (fire, fall, drowning, suffocation, spikes, crushing, freezing, explosion, projectile without an owner, magic, lightning). Chat announces each step. The learning resets at the next raid. Turrets, player weapons, projectiles fired by players and the support cannon are exempt and never get weaker. Raiders are protected from sunlight only: flame arrows, lava and fire traps still hurt, and fire traps adapt like any other class.
* **Job in the game.** Stops one-trap fortresses and nudges the team toward mixed defences and active fighting.

#### Raid music and the music player (0.0.17; songs and order 0.0.19)
* **What the player sees and hears.** When a raid starts, the game's own music stops and a raid song plays for every player in the Overworld. Each wave starts a new song and loops it until the next wave; the change is a 3-second fade. No song plays twice in one raid. The boss wave of a hard raid has its own song, and a special raid opens with its own. A small strip under the beacon's status card shows the song's name with a moving level meter, the time and a progress bar, for the whole raid (the status card itself still hides away from the beacon). Winning plays a short fanfare (the owner's Victory Fanfare, 10 s).
* **Dials.** The pools (`RaidPlaylist.SONGS`), the fades and the quiet after a raid (`RaidMusic`), the loudness of each file (normalised to -16 LUFS by `tools/audio/import_songs.py`; volumes in `sounds.json`). Each player can turn the raid music or the strip off in the beacon's Settings (saved on their computer).
* **Job in the game.** Pillar 5: a raid should feel like an event, and the change of song tells everyone a new wave has started, even far from the beacon.
* **Not played yet.** Nothing was listened to in a real game (the test machine has no sound device); whether a 2-minute song looped through a long wave becomes tiresome; whether the fade between waves is too long or too short.

#### Warnings and announcements
* Chat warns a day ahead, then every six hours, then once more when the raid is only waiting for night. Big banners mark the start, each wave, VICTORY (with confetti) and DEFEAT. A red BEACON IS BEING ATTACKED warning reaches every player in every dimension, with no tool in hand needed; its alarm (a 0.6 s double beep) repeats after a 0.5 s pause for as long as the warning shows.
* **Job in the game.** Pillar 5: readable and teachable. A player should never be surprised by a raid they could have prepared for.

### Part C. Money and trade

#### Ardent Energy
* **What it is.** The soft currency. A glowing item won by fighting; it cannot be crafted.
* **How it behaves.** Since 1.1.4 (owner) only mobs with the red mark drop it: raid mobs and Tactical Operation mobs, at 1.5 times the base chance (1.5 × 6.67%, about one kill in 10; 1.1.5, was twice), raised by the raid's modifiers (each adds its percent of the base chance; all of them make it twice) and by Luck. Looting adds 1 percent per level, mobs with 60 or more health drop two, a raid's boss always drops its share. Ordinary wild mobs drop none; a boss with 300 or more health drops one. Elite mobs always drop their own. Every won raid pays a fixed amount, ceil((1 + tier) × 1.25), raised by the modifiers' payout, plus 1 for each wave with an elite, plus the drops.
* **What it buys.** Pack: flares, the cannon, Exchange goods. Standalone: all of that plus beacon upgrades, repairs and Mess Hall upgrades.
* **Job in the game.** The meter of progress. In the pack, factory parts pay for upgrades, so energy stays for flares, the cannon and trade. In standalone, energy is the only currency for everything.
* **Dials.** `baseDropChance`, `markedDropMultiplier`, `lootingBonusPerLevel`, `bossDrop` in `eternal-defense-common.toml`; `ArdentEnergy.BOSS_HEALTH`; `Economy`, `RaidRewards`, `Rules.raidArdent`.

#### Exchange Shop
* **What it is.** A shop booth placed inside the zone with two tabs: Sell (hand in materials for energy) and Buy (pay energy for goods).
* **How it behaves.** Buying always costs more than selling the same thing, and a unit test checks that no loop pays. Offers live in `config/eternal-defense-exchange.txt` (`[sell]`, `[buy]`), so a server can change them. The list scrolls with the mouse wheel or a scroll bar beside it (1.1.1), and says which offers are shown; an offer whose name does not fit its row reads in full on hover. Pack: the player hands in factory surplus and buys supplies and ammo coins at a premium. Standalone: the player hands in rare vanilla materials (diamond, blaze rod, ender pearl and similar) and buys ammo coins and basic materials. Since 0.0.21 both editions sell iron in bulk (16 iron blocks for 8 energy, a little cheaper per ingot than 16 ingots for 1), and the pack edition also sells iron ingots and the standalone edition's rare materials (gold, blaze rods, ender pearls, diamonds) at the same prices. Since 0.0.41 (owner) a blaze rod costs 6 (8 gold 2, an ender pearl 3, a diamond 6); a world whose exchange file is the untouched 0.0.28 default gets the new one, an edited file is kept. Since 0.0.43 (owner) iron costs twice as much per unit and comes as ore: 16 Iron Ore for 2 (instead of 16 ingots for 1: a furnace makes one ingot of each, a Create crusher about 1.75) and 8 iron blocks for 8 (instead of 16 for 8); 16 andesite alloy cost 1 (instead of 2). An untouched 0.0.41/0.0.42 file moved on in the same way until 1.0.0; since 1.0.0 the shop writes its default only when the file is missing and never converts an older one.
* **Job in the game.** The bridge between a factory player and a non-factory player: one sells surplus, the other buys parts at a premium. Factory is faster by design.

#### Ammo Coins
* **What it is.** A special coin won only from raids; it cannot be crafted.
* **How it behaves.** At the Ammo Platform, Buy with coins pays for the whole batch shown. The coin price grows with the Age and the strength of the gun. Coins buy empty magazines, never filled ones.
* **Job in the game.** Makes ammunition a raid reward, so a won raid replenishes the team (pillar 3).

#### Workshop Fabrication
* **What it is.** A beacon tab selling the starter version of every station: eleven installations: the four tables, Mess Hall, Cook Pot, Bowl Dispenser, Milk Dispenser, Support Platform, Support Cannon and Exchange Shop.
* **How it behaves.** The price comes out of the player's pack at once, with a check that the output fits. Prices live in `Economy`. Each table upgrades itself at its own screen. A table that no installed mod gives anything to make (an empty weapon, ammo, attachment or armor list) is not sold: its card says so instead of its price and the server refuses the order (1.1.3).
* **Job in the game.** One place to learn what exists and what it costs.

### Part D. The workshop tables

#### Weapon, Ammo, Attachment and Armor Platforms
* **What they are.** Four workbench stations. **Weapons**: search by name, filter by type and era, craft any unlocked gun. **Ammo**: only ammo that fits the held gun, paid in materials or Ammo Coins. **Attachments**: scopes, grips, magazines, only parts that fit the held gun; a counter shows how many are locked. **Armor**: helmets, vests, boots, pouches, plates, vision gear. A **Supplies** category in the Weapon table holds food, medical kits, grenades, tools and gear materials.
* **How they behave.** The first Weapon Platform comes from winning the first raid; the others are bought at Workshop Fabrication. A magazine crafted at the Ammo Platform is empty and is filled with loose rounds using the magazine addon. A magazine's Age is its family's earliest gun, and since 0.0.42 (owner) never later than the Age at which the table lists the rounds it holds; extended magazines stay one Age per level above the standard one. Guns that use the FUEL reload type are excluded from crafting (they need Field Radio refills) as a stability fallback.
* **Job in the game.** Converts materials into combat power, with eras gating the pace.
* **Ammo parts (pack edition, 0.0.22; 0.0.26).** An ammo batch of n rounds takes ceil(1.2 x ceil(n / 8)) cartridge cases and 2 x ceil(n / 16) propellant (at least 2 of each): twice the propellant and 20 percent more cases than before, rounded up (`AmmoInputs`). The Universal Ammo Platform sets these itself, replacing any cases and propellant the recipe lists (0.0.26: an older pack script kept the old amounts on the platform); the mod's recipe rules (`RecipeRules`, since 1.0.1; a KubeJS script before) use the same numbers for TaCZ's own recipes. Weak special rounds keep the old price, one case per 8 rounds and one propellant per 16 (`AmmoInputs.SPECIAL`: an ammo id containing flare, bean_bag, pepperball, water or medkit). The standalone edition's ammo costs vanilla materials and is unchanged.
* **Dials.** Recipes and costs in the pack scripts, `AmmoInputs` and `Economy`; per-gun Age in the data.

#### Ages and table upgrades
* **What they are.** Five Ages each table can reach: Frontier, World Wars, Modern, Advanced, Exotic. Each of the four tables has its own Age. A modern pistol stays a Modern unlock.
* **What the player does.** Opens a table, picks the Upgrade tab and presses one big **Upgrade to <Age>** button. A ladder shows the Ages done, the current one and the next; a grid shows the materials needed and what the player holds. The button stays dark until the materials are present.
* **How it behaves.** Modern and later Ages need the team to have visited the Nether (and, in the pack, the Create precision parts). Locks apply in Survival. Creative is free. An ammo or attachment table lists only what fits the gun in hand (since 1.1.1 the attachment table sorts by TaCZ's attachment type, like the armor table's gear types: sights and scopes, muzzles, stocks, grips, lasers, extended mags, other, each with its count); when everything that fits waits in a later Age, the list says so with the count ("There are 3 more attachments for this weapon in later Ages. Upgrade your table"; 0.0.41, back from before the UI redo), and with no gun in hand it says to hold one.
* **Job in the game.** The long progression spine of the gun side of the game, in five steps.
* **Not played yet.** The pace of the Age upgrades against raids.

#### Crafting cost balancer and one-line Ages (1.1.4)
* **What it is.** Owner: "Balance the cost from the damage output, if a weapon in an age is extraordinarily powerful compare to the median of it's peers, add diamond/emerald/blaze rods into the recipe if the recipe from the pack author don't already have it", and any pack is judged by the same standard; moving an item to another Age is one line.
* **How it behaves.** Damage output = shots of one magazine x damage a shot (TaCZ's per-shot damage, the first distance step; a blast adds its damage) / (the time to fire them at the gun's rate, a bolt action waiting for its bolt, + the empty reload); an endless feed counts as 100 rounds. Each Age's median comes from the guns the weapon table lists in it (Ages with fewer than 3 are not judged). A gun at 1.4 times its median or more needs rare parts: 1, plus 1 every 0.8 above, at most 4; each part is 1 diamond (Ages 1, 2), 2 emeralds (Age 3), 3 blaze rods (Age 4) or 4 blaze rods (Age 5). A recipe that already asks for a diamond, emerald or blaze rod is left as its author made it. The rare part is listed first in the gun's costs. The table shows "Strong: 147% of its Age's usual damage" under such a gun (above the unlock lines when the gun is locked; left out in the compact view of a locked gun). Offline, with the owner's pack (14 gun packs), the Age medians were 13.1, 30.4, 40.0, 44.9 and 57.7 damage a second.
* **Ages.** `data/eternal_defense/age_overrides.json` in the mod and `config/eternal-defense-ages.json` on a server: `"id": Age`, one line per item, by item, gun, ammo or recipe id; it wins over every catalogue, at all four tables, and the gun gets its new Age's receiver. A gun that no catalogue knows (a new pack) is placed in the latest Age whose median (of the catalogued guns) it reaches.
* **Attachments.** An add-on pack's attachment that raises damage directly (bullet damage up by addend, percent, multiplier or a script; blast damage up; explosive rounds switched on) is not sold; lowering is fine. TaCZ's own attachments are kept (owner: special cases; `damage_attachments_kept_from` in `balance.json`). An offline read of the gun packs in the owner's pack finds such attachments in six of them (Create Armorer 3, WaT 1, EMX-Arms 32, TRIS-dyna 15, MS-Mobius 25, Helldivers 6); TaCZ's own hollow point (x1.3), incendiary (x1.1) and HE rounds stay.
* **Dials.** `data/eternal_defense/balance.json` (threshold, step, cap, minimum peers, rare items, material and amount by Age, kept packs).

### Part E. The support system (one per player)

#### Support Platform
* **What it is.** A player's own landing pad and supply box, up to four levels (a supply grid from 3 × 3 up to 6 × 6; the pack upgrades cost 16, 32 and 64 plating, standalone prices are in `Economy`).
* **How it behaves.** One per player, only the owner can open it, placed inside the zone, two blocks tall with an owner sign above it. The two blocks in front of it are a no-build **return zone** that must stay clear. Since 0.0.42 (owner) anything solid that gets there without being placed (sand or gravel falling in, a block pushed by a piston or a contraption) is broken out twice a second and drops as an item, with the KEEP RETURN ZONE CLEAR warning to players nearby; the mod's own pieces are never in it (a cannon whose 3 x 3 x 2 would reach into it is refused). It stops working while the beacon is gone. The shovel moves it free.
* **Job in the game.** Personal ownership (every player has something that is theirs) and the anchor for deliveries and returns.

#### Support Cannon
* **What it is.** A big 3 × 3 gun that belongs to its owner. Everything the owner calls goes through it. Bought for 12 Ardent Energy.
* **How it behaves.** It turns toward the flare with a looping turning sound, locks on with a clunk and fires the moment that sound ends (0.0.18). It handles one call at a time. Far-away players read "Cannon preparing" and "You heard cannon fire roaring". Clicking it opens the choice of fire support and the upgrades.
* **Job in the game.** It gives each player a "button" that pays off for energy, and makes the base feel defended by something big.

#### Flares
Flares are bought at the platform's Shop tab (hovering a product shows its whole text and the item's own description, 1.1.1); throwing them is free. If something goes wrong the flare is handed back. When the cannon takes a call the thrower's radio answers with one of the owner's lines; since 0.0.41 a player's radio plays one line at a time (flare calls and Specialty Seller orders share it): a call or an order made while a line still plays is carried out but adds no voice (`SupportChatter.claim`, line lengths measured from the files).

| Flare | Price (energy) | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply | 3 | The cannon fires and a chest with everything in the platform's grid parachutes in (about 12 s outside, 7 s underground). The grid empties when sent. |
| Return | 2 | A portal (30 s, 5 min and usable twice with the upgrade) takes the owner to the platform from any dimension. It needs a free cell in front of the platform, or it is cancelled. |

When the cannon takes a flare's call the thrower's radio answers with one of the owner's lines for that flare: six for fire support, two for supply and three for return (0.0.22).
| Fire Support | 6 | A red box marks the target and the cannon shells it with the fire support the owner chose. A second flare during a barrage waits ("Barrage in process"). |
| Loadout (1.1.0) | 5 | The cannon fires a capsule with the kit of the thrower's active Loadout coin; since 1.1.1 it plunges from the sky with no parachute, lands with a blast that breaks nothing, stands up facing them and opens by itself. Needs a Loadout Station and a coin; the coin is used only when the cannon fires. |

* **Job in the game.** The three things a player in a raid wants most: a resupply, an escape, and a big explosion, each at a small price that is affordable after two or three raids.

#### Twelve fire support types and ten cannon upgrades
* **Types.** Explosion Barrage (6 shells), Spotter Arrows (6 volleys; the Arrow Cluster until 0.0.36), Narukami's Favor (lightning on every hostile, 5), Bunker Buster (one bomb digging a large cube; it hurts players inside its box for half their health and is handed back if its box would touch the beacon zone), Healing Barrage, Curse of Debilitation (debuffs until death since 0.0.36), and since 0.0.16: Cluster Strike (bomblets over a much wider area, 5), Cryo Shell (freezes hostiles nearly in place, 4), Napalm Carpet (burning ground that sets hostiles Ablaze until they die, 4), Gravity Well (drags hostiles together, then collapses, 3), Shockwave (throws hostiles away from the flare, 4) and Starshell (one light for 30 s: hostiles glow, players see in the dark). The owner's choice applies even to a borrowed flare.
* **How a shot looks (0.0.16).** The cannon fires up into the sky; nothing flies from it to the target. Each round is seen falling from high above the flare (48 blocks in 0.0.16, 80 since 0.0.18; a shell banded in the type's colour, or the Bunker Buster's bomb), with the owner's incoming scream played where it will land (0.0.18, back in 0.0.20; the 0.0.19 whistle in between), and lands when the flare resolves the shot; a flare underground (Quantum tunneling) is reached the same way, through the rock. Big blasts are seen and heard up to 160 blocks away and shake the view of nearby players.
* **Upgrades.** Traverse, Fire Rate, Volley, Damage, Quantum Tunneling (works underground), Slowness field, a lasting two-way Portal, Healing Aura, Area of Effect and Dimensional Link (works in other dimensions). About 185 energy in total in standalone; the pack pays the factory parts listed in `Economy.PACK_AMOUNTS`.
* **Job in the game.** The long energy sink after the first purchases, and a way to specialise a cannon (damage dealer, healer, controller).

### Part F. The kitchen

#### The idea
A fortress should have a kitchen feeding the war effort. A farmer or cook supports the team with portable sandwiches and big pots of stew before a raid. Casual cooking is useful; a dedicated cook supports more people without being mandatory.

#### Mess Hall (four levels)
* **What it is.** The cooking station, a 2 × 1 × 2 block placed inside the zone. It has up to six shared food slots, a bread-only **Base** slot and a sandwich output slot.
* **What the player does.** Opens it, clicks the effects to choose on the right, presses Gather ingredients, then Make sandwich or Cook stew. Buttons that do things are on the left and the effects to choose are on the right.
* **What each level adds.**

| Level | Makes | Meals last | Food slots | Doubling (×2) | Pots linked | Stew servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mk I | sandwiches only | 15 minutes | 3 | no | 1 | 3 |
| Mk II | sandwiches and stew | 20 minutes | 3 | no | 2 | 5 |
| Mk III | sandwiches and stew | 25 minutes | 3 | yes | 3 | 7 |
| Mk IV | sandwiches and stew | 30 minutes | 6 | yes, and legendary effects too | 4 | 9 |

* **Prices.** The hall itself is crafted from vanilla materials or bought at Workshop Fabrication. Upgrades cost 8 / 16 / 32 Reinforced Beacon Plating (pack) or 4 / 8 / 14 Ardent Energy (standalone); Creative is free; food and pot links stay.
* **What the screen says.** Three chips under the order show the meal length, the open food slots and whether ×2 is unlocked (a padlock until Mk III). The Stew tab shows a padlock below Mk II. The Upgrade button's tooltip says what the next level adds.
* **Job in the game.** A visible ladder of four clear steps, each unlocking one thing the player can feel: a first meal, stew for the team, double effects, and the full six-slot table.
* **Dials.** `MealRules.tier` (minutes, slots, stew, doubling, pots, servings), `Economy` for prices.
* **Not played yet.** Whether 15, 20, 25 and 30 minutes and the Mk III doubling step feel right in play.

#### Cook Pot and stew
* **What it is.** A 1 × 1 × 2 pot with a chalkboard menu on it. One selected pot, linked to the hall within 8 blocks, holds a batch of stew with up to three effects and 3 to 9 servings. A lamp bar above the chalkboard gives some light (block light 10) and the board text is drawn full-bright, so the menu can be read in a dark kitchen.
* **How it behaves.** A cook consumes food units (12 / 21 / 30 / 36 at Mk I to IV since 0.0.35; they were 4 / 7 / 10 / 12) spread over stacks, including one of every food type used. A bowl right-click takes one serving and eats it at once; the bowl is used up (0.0.37, owner: a sink for bowls; creative keeps it, as vanilla). Cooking into a pot that still has stew is allowed after a confirmation ("Replace the stew?": the old stew is thrown away, *Keep it* changes nothing, Escape keeps it); the server refuses a plain cook into a pot with stew, only the confirmed button replaces it. A Mk I hall cannot cook stew.
* **Job in the game.** Communal pre-raid meals: one cook feeds the whole team.

#### Sandwich
* **What it is.** A portable meal for one player, stacking to 16, eaten at any hunger level.
* **How it behaves.** Two bread in the Base slot plus two or three different fillings, two of each (0.0.35). It gives one or two effects. The base bread gives nothing.
* **Job in the game.** The casual, personal option for solo play and for cooks without a pot.

#### Ingredients, groups and effects
* **Food groups.** Six: Protein and dairy, Grain and staples, Fruit and sweets, Fish, Vegetables, Fungi and fermented. Every vanilla food has a group and traits; the kitchen tooltips list which foods feed which effect.
* **Everyday effects (11).** Vitality (+4 health), Fortitude (+2 armor), Steadiness (+5 percent knockback resistance), Hardy (15 percent slower hunger, 0.0.35), Might (+8 percent melee damage), Agility (+8 percent attack speed), Fortune (+1 luck), Recovery (heals 1 health every 5 seconds), Blast Ward (20 percent explosion protection, 0.0.35), Projectile Ward (15 percent protection from arrows, bolts, fireballs and tridents, 0.0.35), Strong Swimmer (+10 percent swim speed).
* **Legendary effects (15).** Each needs foods from two specific groups, one recipe for every pair of the six groups: Firepower, Quick Hands, Brawler, Heavy Hand, Demolition, Recoil Control, Focus, Snap Aim, Fast Draw, Dead Eye, Gun Mobility, Impact, Rapid Fire, Scoped Focus and Hip Focus. They improve gun damage, reload, recoil, accuracy, aiming and draw speed, bash damage and knockback, grenade radius, headshots, movement with a gun and rate of fire (the exact percentages are in section 5.8 and in the guide).
* **Rules.** A legendary effect uses up both of its groups, so those groups cannot also feed everyday effects, and two legendary picks cannot share a group. A sandwich holds at most two effects, a stew at most three. A Mk IV stew with three separate pairs, which uses all six groups and all six food slots, makes three legendary effects at once.
* **Job in the game.** Ordinary effects are survival stats; legendary effects are gun stats that ask the cook to plan around groups. The two together make the cook a real specialist.

#### Doubling (×2)
* **What it is.** A way to make one effect count twice.
* **How it behaves.** Put two different foods of the same group on the table that both feed the effect (for example cooked chicken and cooked mutton for Vitality). One food is not enough, and two stacks of the same food do not count. Unlocked at Mk III, in sandwiches and in stew. A legendary effect needs two different foods from each of its two groups, four food slots, so only Mk IV can double one. A doubled effect shows ×2 on its order card.
* **Job in the game.** The reason to invest in a higher hall and to vary the pantry. It rewards cooks who stock a wider range of food.
* **Designer watch-out.** The Mk IV legendary doubling was the hardest rule to explain; the screen now shows the ×2 requirement per recipe in its tooltip (open, locked at Mk III, or locked at Mk IV).

#### Gather, Take back, Clear order
* **What they do.** Gather lays out exactly the foods that make the chosen effects, taking them from the pack and (0.0.40) the Home Zone's Logistics Hubs (it spends nothing; only cooking does). Since 0.0.41 the hall also counts the Hubs' food when it shows what can be cooked, so with the food only in a Hub the player opens the hall, picks effects, presses Gather and cooks. Take back puts the whole table back in the pack (never into a Hub). Clear order empties the choices. Choosing nothing lets the hall pick effects from whatever food is on the table.
* **Job in the game.** The player thinks in effects ("I want Vitality and Fortitude"), not in recipes and stacks.

#### The active meal
* **How it behaves.** A player has one active meal. A new meal replaces the old one. Eaten meals show as ordinary potion effects (value and time), plus a Home Zone effect. A meal lasts as long as the hall that made it allows (15 to 30 field minutes). Logging out pauses it. **Death clears it**, and milk clears it too. Inside the base zone ("home") every value doubles and the timer stops; stepping out resumes it. Returning home never refreshes the time.
* **Job in the game.** Makes the base the best place to fight (the cook's buffs are strongest there) while meals still travel with the player, for a limited time.

#### Bowl Dispenser and Milk Dispenser
* **What they are.** Two utility blocks. The Bowl Dispenser (1 × 1 × 2) gives out bowls: right-click takes one, and crouching with empty hands opens its stock (up to 64 bowls). The Milk Dispenser holds 8,000 mB of milk (buckets add 1,000, bottles 250) and a normal use spends 250 mB, clearing every potion effect and the active meal.
* **How they behave.** Each announces what it did (bowls left, what milk cleared, milk left). Milk is not spent if there is nothing to clear. Milk Bottles carry the same cleanse.
* **Job in the game.** Bowls remove tedium from serving stew; milk is the "reset" for a meal the player no longer wants.

### Part F2. The drinks corner (0.0.35)

* **What it is.** The Mess Hall's sibling for potions. The Drink Station (Mk I to IV, the owner's models, 2 wide x 1 deep x 2 tall) is a brewing stand with a 54-slot potion storage; from Mk II it mixes potions into drinks. Drinks go into Brew Pots (one serving each) or into a Latte to carry.
* **How it behaves.** The brewing stand is vanilla's (Forge's brewing registry, so other mods' recipes brew there); a finished bottle with an effect that the ingredient in the stand cannot brew further moves into the storage when there is room. The storage holds any potion (drinkable, splash, lingering) as a count, 64 of one kind a slot; clicking takes one bottle, shift takes as many as the player can carry; shift-clicking a potion in the pack stores it. A station holding more than 256 bottles refuses to be broken by hand.
* **Mixing.** Mk II/III/IV: 1/2/3 effects per drink and 1/2/3 linked Brew Pots within 8 blocks. One potion per effect; a potion mixes only when it is drinkable, has exactly one effect, and that effect is beneficial with a duration; Regeneration goes in the sweetener instead. The potion's amplifier is kept, its own time is ignored: a drink lasts 10 minutes in the field. Gather takes the strongest potion of each chosen effect from the storage first, then the pack, and spends nothing; Take back returns them. Brewing over a pot that still holds a drink asks first.
* **Sweetener.** A 4,000 mB tank filled from drinkable Regeneration potions (Regeneration I, also the long one, 250 mB; Regeneration II 750 mB; the bottle comes back). Each +10 minutes costs 1,000 mB (+0, +10 or +20; 10 to 30 minutes); only full steps are spent. No sweetener for the Latte.
* **The screen.** Left: the brewing stand with its fuel bar, then the storage grid with each slot's count. Right: the tabs Brew Pot and Latte, a one-line notice strip, one row of effect tiles (paged past 11), the Brew Pot chips (a pot, its number, the drink's colour and a bar, like the Mess Hall's stew pot chips), the mixing row (the pot or the cup, the mixing slots, then the sweetener slot with its graduated bar and the +0/+10/+20 button, or the Latte's output slot), a preview line (effects, levels, time), Gather or Brew, Take back, Clear, then the pack.
* **Drinking.** Right-click a full Brew Pot with a Paper Cup: the drink is drunk at once and the cup is used up (0.0.37, owner: a sink for cups; creative keeps it, as vanilla; it was kept before).
* **Latte.** A Paper Cup (used up) plus up to 2 mixable potions (1 at Mk II): 10 minutes, stacks to 16, drunk whenever, leaves nothing.
* **The active drink.** Its own slot beside the meal; a new drink replaces the old. Timer only in the field; inside the Home Zone or an ACTIVE FOB zone it stops and the effects show as endless; no doubling. Logout pauses it, death clears it, the Milk Dispenser and the Milk Bottle clear it, the vanilla milk bucket does not. The effects are real vanilla effects: the tracker recognises its own by level and expected time, never removes or extends another, lets an equal or stronger potion run out first (the drink keeps counting and comes back), and gives back a weaker potion that vanilla's stacking dropped under the drink. When something else keeps taking an endless effect away at home (two losses of one effect within 30 s; drink, meal or zone effect alike), that effect shows at home as a 20-minute timer, topped up in place every minute, instead of endless (0.0.37, `EndlessEffects`).
* **Prices.** Upgrades to Mk II/III/IV: 8/16/32 Reinforced Beacon Plating (pack) or 4/8/14 Ardent Energy (standalone). Workshop Fabrication (Kitchen group): Drink Station Mk I (4 plating or Ardent Energy, 8 iron, a brewing stand), Brew Pot (14 copper, a sign), Cup Dispenser (8 iron, 4 glass, 4 paper). Crafting: Station Mk I (4 planks, 2 iron, a glass bottle, a brewing stand, a cauldron), Brew Pot (7 copper, a sign, a cauldron), 4 Paper Cups from 3 paper.

### Part F3. The MRE Station (0.0.38)

* **What it is.** A ration packer for the kitchen: one block, no levels, 1 x 1 x 2 (the owner's model; the upper cell is the invisible filler, as the pots and dispensers). It turns food the player picks into one MRE to carry.
* **The MRE.** It holds exactly what its foods give, no gain and no loss. Nutrition is the sum of the foods' nutrition. Saturation is the sum of what each food adds to saturation when eaten alone, by vanilla's own formula (nutrition x saturation modifier x 2, in float as vanilla), stored as the plain sum, at most 20 (`MreRules`). One MRE holds any total up to 20 nutrition; one food is enough. It is eaten through vanilla's own path (`Player.eat` -> `FoodData.eat` with food properties built from the stored totals and the modifier that gives the stored saturation back), so vanilla's saturation cap (the food bar) applies as for any food. It is eaten in 16 ticks, only when the player is hungry (vanilla's rule; creative players can always eat), restores the whole amount at once and leaves nothing behind. MREs with the same totals have the same tag and stack to 64.
* **Food only.** Any item with food properties goes in, modded ones too, except an MRE. The foods are never eaten, so none of their own code runs: every effect is dropped (golden apples, honey's cure, suspicious stew, rotten flesh, raw chicken, pufferfish, spider eye, poisonous potato, chorus fruit's teleport), and so is always-edible. A Prepared Sandwich loses its meal. A food with negative nutrition or a broken modifier is refused.
* **The screen.** Six food slots (3 x 2), Marines Fuel over Paper (the "seal"), an arrow and the output; a card with the live totals of one MRE (Nutrition N / 20 with a mark per point, Saturation S with its bar); Make MRE and Take back; one line that says what is missing or "Ready to pack 1 MRE." (and how many containers come back). Holding a food over a food slot previews it, in red when it would pass 20; that slot refuses it. Tooltips on the empty slots, the totals and a disabled Make explain why.
* **Making.** Each MRE takes one of each food in the slots (a slot may hold a stack, as in a crafting grid; since 1.1.1 each slot must hold a different food, so one stack cannot be spread over the six slots for a full 20), 1 Marines Fuel and 1 Paper. The server checks everything again on Make (only food, at most 20, at least 1 nutrition, fuel, paper, an output that is empty or holds MREs with the same totals and room) and then makes it in one step; nothing is spent when it refuses, and it says why in the action bar. The foods' containers come back to the pack, or drop at the player's feet when it is full: a food's crafting remainder (the honey bottle's glass bottle, most modded bowls) or the stews' bowl. Take back returns everything in the station. Breaking the station drops what it holds.
* **Marines Fuel.** A wax crayon, the binder that seals the paper. A clay ball and any dye (`forge:dyes`) make 2; it stacks to 64 and is not food.
* **Price.** Workshop Fabrication, the Kitchen group's 8th card (right column, bottom): 4 Reinforced Beacon Plating (pack) or 4 Ardent Energy (standalone), 8 iron ingots and a smoker (`Economy.MRE_STATION_KEY`, `MRE_STATION_IRON`). No crafting recipe.
* **Float detail.** Vanilla's formula multiplies in float, so for some totals no modifier gives the stored saturation back to the last bit; then the nearest one below is used (in a test of 20,000 random packs of vanilla foods, 727 land one float step, about a millionth, below the stored value; never above).

### Part G. The two gun add-on mods

#### Gun Guide
* **What it is.** A client-only card that lists the controls of the gun in the player's hand, with the player's own key bindings.
* **How it behaves.** Draw a gun and the card opens for five seconds, then shrinks to a badge; a key (configurable) toggles it. A warning appears if two actions share a key. A red JAM alert appears when the gun jams (this needs the TaCZ Durability addon).
* **Job in the game.** Removes the biggest barrier to using many different guns: not knowing the controls.

#### Gun Displays
* **What it is.** Eleven decoration blocks for guns: stands, wall racks, glass cases; and the Weapon Display Tuner (1.1.2).
* **How it behaves.** Since 1.1.2, with a gun in hand one press of TaCZ's interact key puts it on display or swaps it with the gun there (the stands are on TaCZ's `interact_key/whitelist` tag; wide halves' outlines meet in the middle, the heavy stand's outline covers its gun); a right-click with empty hands takes it back; a right-click with a gun only aims. Every stand has its own crafting recipe with vanilla items; with Eternal Defense installed they can also be bought in the Weapon Platform's Supplies category. Wall racks need a solid wall. They count like any other placed block for the base score; there is no special rule for them.
* **The tuner (1.1.2).** Right-click a stand that holds a gun: a side panel sets the gun's size (25% to 300%), its position (up to 16 pixels each way on three axes) and its angle (tilt, turn, roll, up to 180 degrees). Changes show at once; Copy/Paste carry them to another stand; they belong to the stand and stay when guns are swapped. The server accepts them only from a player holding a tuner within 8 blocks, for a stand with a gun, and clamps every number (`DisplayTuning`).
* **Job in the game.** A trophy room. Decoration with no gameplay effect.
* **Not played yet.** Whether the owner wants displays to count more toward the base score.

### Part H. Teaching and teamwork

#### Starter kit
A new player receives a Defense Beacon, the Field Guide, the Recovery Shovel and one starter gun with ammo, once, the first time they join. Since 1.1.3 the gun is TaCZ's own .22 Model 943 revolver (`tacz:taurus943`, 8 rounds, unloaded) with 64 rounds of .22 WMR (`tacz:22wmr`), so it exists with TaCZ alone (until 1.1.2: an FK15P flintlock from a gun pack and 64 round balls).

#### Field Guide
* **What it is.** The in-game book. Fourteen pages with a side menu that shows an item icon per page; a Reference tab holds the exact numbers.
* **How it behaves.** Every feature is a card: what it is, what it gives, how it works, how to unlock it, and red *Careful* notes for catches (the beacon only comes off with the shovel and that resets the campaign). It is written for a player who has just spawned in and is not the design document. The same tone applies to tooltips, confirmations and hints. Tests fail if a card is out of format, a registered block or item has no card, or a number in the guide stops matching the code.
* **Job in the game.** Pillar 5. Writing rules: `docs/GUIDE-STYLE.md`.

#### Playing together
* **What is shared.** The beacon, its upgrades, the raid level cap and the reward chest.
* **What is personal.** Each player's Support Platform, Cannon, fire support choice and active meal.
* **Designer watch-out.** One player's shovel can reset everyone's campaign. Two-human co-op has not been played yet.

#### Operator commands
`/eternaldefense claim`, `upgrade <branch>`, `repair`; operators also have `boundary`, `test-raid [type]` and `next-raid <type>` to test raid types. Since 1.1.5 operators (level 2) have test commands for the raid features:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `elite <kind> [level] [mob]` | An elite 6 blocks in front of you (kinds: badass, speedster, healer, matrioska, long_shot, flak_armor, body_armor, vampiric, frostbite, berserker; level 1 to 5 for Badass; any mob id, a zombie by default, a skeleton for a Long Shot). During a raid it joins the raid; otherwise it is an ordinary mob that stays. Says its health, size and drops. |
| `raid-mod list` / `clear` / `special <type>` / `<modifier>` | Shows, clears, picks the special raid (normal, air, paratroopers, siege, swarm) or switches one modifier for the next raid, anywhere, between raids. |
| `raid-timer <seconds>` | The next raid is due in that many seconds (bought break time is cleared); it still waits for night and a player near the beacon, and says how to get there (`/time set 14000`). |
| `raid-info` | The next raid (or the running one): level, waves, mobs of wave 1, elites, modifiers, the Ardent drop chance of a red-marked mob and the pay; in a raid also the wave, mobs left to spawn and alive, and the elites alive. |
| `balance [gun]` | The Age medians and the guns far above theirs (top 20 in chat, all in the log), or one gun's Age, damage a second, ratio and costs. |
| `ages [reload]` | The Ages set by hand; `reload` reads `config/eternal-defense-ages.json` again (the tables use it at once, the recipe rules after `/reload`). |

### Part I. Tactical Operations (0.0.20)

#### Command Table and Satellite Beacon
* **What they are.** A war table (2 x 2 x 2, the owner's model) and a satellite dish (1 x 2 x 1, Mk I to III), both bought at Workshop Fabrication, both only inside the zone, one of each per base, moved free with the shovel. The satellite links to the table when it stands within 8 blocks of it, inside the zone, under open sky (computed when needed, never saved). Clicking the satellite says its Mk and its link, or what breaks it.
* **FOB management (0.0.31).** The table's third tab, FOBs, lists every FOB: its name, how far and which way from the base, its villagers, its state and its supply route's light. During an SOS the calling FOB comes first with the time left to answer and a Reprint SOS map button (one copy at a time; it burns when the SOS ends). Both maps show each FOB's zone and a flag with its name; a FOB beyond the map's edge is an arrow at the edge pointing to it, with its name and distance.
* **Job in the game.** A reason to leave the base between raids, as a team, and a second income that does not depend on the raid schedule. The satellite's Mk buys choice (more offers, harder classes, reach), never a higher price for the same class.

#### Hunting missions
* **What they are.** The table's board offers warbands out in the world (2, 3 or 4 offers by satellite Mk; Patrol from Mk I, Garrison from Mk II, Warlord at Mk III), each with a codename ("OP IRON HOUND"), a place and a pay. One mission runs at a time for the whole server.
* **How they behave.** Accept at the table (not during a raid nor in the last day before one: RAID WARNING). Everyone online gets a map with a red X and one Return Flare. The mission line on the status card shows only while a player holds the map or the Recovery Shovel (0.0.21). The warband appears when someone comes within 64 blocks of the X (0.0.21): it needs only one block to stand on within 16 blocks of the X, a small glowing zone (6 blocks) forms around that block, and the enemies come out of red gates just outside it and rush to its middle. With no block at all, everyone there gets a return gate home for 5 minutes and the offer is replaced. Kill all of it and the pay lands in the beacon's reward chest, once (at least 1 Ardent Energy and 2 ammo coins at any reward tier, 0.0.22), and the maps burn. If everybody stays away for 5 minutes it scatters (nothing paid; it comes again on approach). Abandon puts the offer back on the board. A finished offer is replaced at the next dawn, one a day.
* **Job in the game.** Ardent Energy and ammo coins for teams that go out and fight on their own terms; the danger and pay grow with the reward tier, so it never outpaces the raids.

### Part J. Forward Operating Bases (0.0.23 to 0.0.27, slices 1 to 4)

#### FOB Beacon
* **What it is.** A flag post (the owner's FOB station model, 1 x 2 x 1) planted in a vanilla village, which becomes an outpost of the base shared by every player. Up to 5, one per village, Overworld only; no teleport between them and no remote use of the home platform.
* **How it behaves.** Planting calls a vanilla raid on the village at once, at Bad Omen I (the lowest; 3, 5 or 7 waves by difficulty). Win it and the FOB is ACTIVE; lose it (the village falls, or fewer than half of its villagers survive) and it is DOWN. A raid that ends with no result (Peaceful, a timeout) leaves it PLANTED, and Call the raid on its screen starts another. Outside its own raid, the FOB's villagers cannot be hurt. While ACTIVE it sells flares at the Support Platform's prices (the flares still need the player's own platform and cannon at home), and meals last in its village as at home (the FOB Zone effect). DOWN works for nothing; until slice 3 adds repairs, the shovel takes it back and it can be planted again. Raiders of village raids drop no Ardent Energy; the beacon's own raiders still do.
* **Job in the game.** A reason to travel and a forward stop for hunts and exploration: flares and meals far from home, bought with a hard first fight and villagers worth keeping alive. Slice 4 (0.0.27): the Tactical Enchanting Table at home, whose higher levels need ACTIVE FOBs.
* **Seeing where it counts (0.0.30).** Holding a FOB Beacon and looking at the ground shows a see-through FOB, the part of the village it would take as an outline (green when it may be planted, orange when not) and a line under the crosshair: "FOB here: 5 villagers (3 needed) | beds 9 | workstations 4", or why not. Since 0.0.42 (owner) the FOB and its box are red when a block or a creature is in the way of its two cells (a hole one block high), the line says "Something is in the way", and the FOB Beacon is not placed. A planted FOB's zone shows as an olive outline to everyone nearby, so players know where to stand for the meal bonus and the shop; its screen has a Zone outline switch. The owner found a village whose houses stood far apart and whose villagers roamed, and could not tell what a FOB would count.

#### Supply Delivery Gate and Supply Receiving Gate (0.0.24)
* **What they are.** Two gantries (the owner's 0.0.30 models, 4 wide, 2 tall, 1 deep; 0.0.24's were 3 x 3 arches over one rail) across two side-by-side rail lanes: the Supply Delivery Gate stands in a FOB's village, the Supply Receiving Gate in the base zone. Each FOB has one pair. Their nameplates show the FOB's name (UNLINKED when it is gone), and a signal on each column shows how the route is doing.
* **How they behave.** The best line is a loop: two rails side by side through both gates, joined by a curve at each end, so a cart goes out on one and comes home on the other without stopping (one rail ridden there and back also works). Ride a plain minecart from the Receiving Gate through the Delivery Gate once: the route is open, and the FOB sends its tribute into the Receiving Gate's storage by itself, with no cart and no rail needed. Ride a whole round trip (home, the FOB, home) to carry up to two deliveries at once and to renew the route's inspection, which runs for 8 hours of play. Green: all is well. Amber: a ride under way, the inspection running out, or the storage full. Red: nothing moves; the gate's screen says why and how to fix it.
* **Job in the game.** A steady trickle of crafting materials and food for having FOBs, more for a team that builds a fast rail and rides it, and a reason to keep coming back to the line.

#### Tribute and the Receiving Gate's storage (0.0.31)
* **What it is.** Each FOB has four loot slots: Emeralds (every village sends them), The mine, The farm, and the village's Specialty (Fishing & Animal Husbandry (one specialty since 0.0.34), Potion makers, Wandering trader hub or, since 0.0.32, Monster hunter guild, rolled when the FOB is planted). The mine, the farm and the specialty each send the one item the players chose on either gate's screen. Everything lands in the Receiving Gate's storage, a double chest that players can only take from.
* **How it behaves.** A slot can be switched off; the slots still on share its part, so a FOB can focus on the one thing the base needs. Far routes, more villagers and the far bonus (up to +20 percent at 5,000 blocks) make every delivery bigger; cheap items come by the stack, rare ones (a ghast tear, a sapling from the trader) take several deliveries each. The gate's screen shows, live, what each slot sends, how many deliveries one item takes, and the countdown to the next delivery. A full storage pauses deliveries.
* **Job in the game.** The owner felt the old tribute was a random pool. Now the base decides what each outpost works on, a far line pays more, and a village's specialty makes some outposts worth more than others.

#### Village research (0.0.32)
* **What it is.** A research bar per FOB, on the FOB Beacon's Research tab. Players give it emeralds, diamonds or Ardent Energy. Its 5 levels open the specialty's hard-to-get items (the A tier at level 2, the S tier at level 4) and make the slots send more (the specialty +25 percent a level, emeralds, the mine and the farm +5 percent).
* **How it behaves.** Every item counts its points (an emerald 1, Ardent Energy 2, a diamond 4); the bar needs 240 points for level 5. Locked items show a padlock in the gate's grid. Everyone reads a chat line when a level is reached. Even the slowest FOB (3 villagers, gates 128 blocks apart, all four slots on) makes one S-tier item every 30 minutes of play once level 4 is reached.
* **Job in the game.** A sink for emeralds and diamonds that turns an outpost into a source of endgame materials, such as the ender pearls the pack's recipes need from a monster hunter guild.

#### SOS and repairs (0.0.25)
* **What it is.** Every 10 to 12 hours of play one of your ACTIVE FOBs calls for help. Everyone reads it, sees it on the status card and gets a map with a red X on the FOB.
* **How it behaves.** Get there within 7 days of play (the Command Table's FOBs tab shows the time left and reprints a lost map): the raid starts as soon as someone stands in the village. Win it and nothing changes; lose it, or stay away, and the FOB goes DOWN: no shop, no tribute, no route. Repair it at its FOB Beacon for Ardent Energy: the price lists every villager lost in its raids, who come back with their trades.
* **Job in the game.** A reason to keep coming back to your outposts, a cost for neglecting them, and a fight that is about protecting villagers rather than only killing raiders.

#### Supply Catapult and the specialty seller (0.0.33)
* **What it is.** A wooden onager placed in a FOB's village, one per FOB. At research level 5 the FOB's specialty seller, reached from the Command Table's FOBs tab, sells every item of that specialty for Ardent Energy.
* **How it behaves.** Contact Specialty Seller (a handheld radio on the button; radio static as the line opens). Pick an item, buy a lot (one S-tier item's worth) or four; the seller's radio answers. The seller shows the catapult live (0.0.34): it turns toward the Defense Beacon and throws the box. The buyer's chat counts down 12 seconds, as for a supply drop, and the box parachutes down beside the beacon from high above. It holds four boxes; a catapult nobody is near throws all the same. The FOB screen says whether it is ONLINE or OFFLINE and why.
* **Job in the game.** The endgame use of a fully researched FOB: what the deliveries bring slowly can be bought at once, which matters most for the pack's many ender eyes (the monster hunter guild's ender pearls).

#### Tactical Enchanting Table and Warlord Seal (0.0.27)
* **What it is.** A war desk at home (the owner's enchanting bench model, 2 wide, 2 tall, 1 deep) that enchants gear for Ardent Energy: no experience, no lapis, no books, no anvil. One per base, in the zone, bought at Workshop Fabrication. The Warlord Seal is a war trophy from the hardest hunt.
* **How it behaves.** Put a weapon, tool or armor in its slot: every enchant that fits is listed with a button per level and its price. Level I is always open; each higher level needs more ACTIVE FOBs, and the top level of an enchant needs all five. Upgrading the table (Mk I to III) takes 17, 33, then 50 percent off every enchant for everyone. The pack pays twice the standalone price; a pack table at Mk III charges the standalone price (0.0.28). Treasure enchants (Mending, Frost Walker, Soul Speed, Swift Sneak) stay locked until someone spends a Warlord Seal on them; curses are never sold. Vanilla enchanting tables, librarians and anvils still work as always.
* **Job in the game.** The payoff for holding many FOBs: the best enchants without grinding experience, a sink for Ardent Energy, and a reason to take on Warlord hunts.

### Part K. The Logistics Center Hub and the supply source (0.0.40)
* **What it is.** A big supply depot for the base: one 3 x 3 x 3 block (the owner's model) with 270 slots, inside the Home Zone. It is the base's shared store: stations take from it, and factory lines fill it.
* **How it behaves.** Placed from its item on the ground in front of the player (the 27 cells must be free and inside the Home Zone). Right-click any of its blocks to open a chest-style screen of 54 slots a page, 5 pages (the arrows in the title bar), with "N / 270 slots used". Shift-click from the pack fills the whole Hub, the same kind first. Hoppers, droppers and Create's machines put items in and take them out on every face.
* **The supply source.** Any recipe, upgrade, purchase, repair or Gather made in the Home Zone takes from the pack first, then from the Hubs (nearest first); if the whole price is not there, nothing is taken. When a Hub pays part of it, the player and everyone in the Home Zone read one line saying what came from the Hub.
* **Job in the game.** The end of the factory loop: a Create line can deliver its materials straight into the Hub, and the players spend them without carrying them. It is the dearest installation, so it comes after a few raids.
* **What can go wrong.** Outside the Home Zone (or with no beacon standing) it does nothing and says so; its items wait inside. It cannot be mined while it holds items.
* **Where it reaches (0.0.41, owner).** A player standing in the Home Zone of a base with at least one working Hub gets the **Logistics Hub Zone** effect (display only, like the Home Zone effect, its text "You are in the Logistics Hub zone."; checked once a second, `HubZone`); it goes when they leave or no Hub works. The beacon's Overview says "This base has N active Logistics Hubs" or "This base does not have any active Logistics Hub".

### Part L. The Loadout Station (1.1.0)
* **What it is.** A 3 x 3 x 3 machine for the base (the owner's model) that dresses a player in a saved kit in one go: armor, off hand and the whole hotbar row. The kit is stored in a **Loadout Coin**, made at the station. A **Loadout Flare** brings the same kit to wherever the player is, in a capsule that plunges from the sky.
* **How it behaves.** Right-click the station to open its screen; sneak and right-click it to print the coin of your active slot at once (since 1.1.1; until then a click on the doors printed). The screen is a closet: on the left the player's own figure, lit on a slowly turning stand (drag to turn it), wears the kit of the selected coin slot, or the kit being packed while the pack area holds something; < and > step through the 9 slots like hangers. On the right: the pack area (4 armor slots, the off hand, the 9 hotbar slots: put a kit in), the fee, **Make coin**, and, between the closet and the pack area, the wardrobe: a column of 9 numbered coin slots on a brass rail (the selected row lit, the active one tagged Active, a click on a row shows it in the closet) shared by everyone at the base (each slot remembers the kind of coin it holds and takes only identical coins), **Set active** (your own active slot), **Print** and the Locker page. The Loadout Capsule's screen shows the same figure wearing what is inside the capsule.
* **The print.** The player is walked in (the doors swing open as they come), held still in the machine for a 4-second cycle (doors shut, the ring drops and the arms work the player over, sparks at the nozzles; at the middle of the cycle, in one step, what they wear and hold in those 14 slots is stowed and the kit is put on), then walked out to the cell 2 blocks in front of the doors, where the doors close behind them. A camera shows it from behind, then from inside the machine looking at the player, then pulls back into the player's eyes (sneak or Esc skips it; a client setting turns it off). The 27 main inventory slots are never touched.
* **Job in the game.** The base's armory: after a death or before a raid, one click puts a whole fighting kit back on, gun with its attachments and loaded magazine included; the flare does it in the field. It turns the factory's output into ready kits and gives the Hub a reason to store spare gear.
* **What can go wrong.** It refuses before anything changes when the station is busy (it names who), the player is not in front of the doors within 4 blocks with a clear path, the exit cell is blocked, the 30-second cooldown runs, there is no coin, or there is no room to stow what they wear (Hubs, then the station's 27-slot Locker). A print stopped before the kit goes on (the player leaves, dies, changes dimension, or the station is broken) uses nothing. It cannot be mined while coins wait in its slots ("Empty the machine first").

## 5. Systems (rulebook with exact numbers)

### 5.1 Defense Beacon and base zone
* One beacon per world; its zone is a glowing grid. Core level sets the radius: **8, 12, 18, 24** blocks (Mk-1 to Mk-4).
* Upgrade branches: **Core, Logistics, Defense, Restoration, Reconnaissance, Vertical.** Standalone upgrade prices in Ardent Energy: **4, 8, 14, 22, 32** by level. Pack prices are factory parts (`Rules.upgradeCost`: 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 of the branch's part). Repair costs 6 (standalone) or 8 (pack).
* Commands for players: `/eternaldefense claim`, `upgrade <branch>`, `repair`; operators: `boundary`, `test-raid [type]`, `next-raid <type>`.
* **Reward chest:** the beacon is the chest (54-slot menu from the Overview's reward button). No block is placed; overflow stays queued; contents spill out if the beacon is destroyed.
* **Base score** (structure, palette, details, lighting, furnishings, layout, machinery, weapon stations) sets the reward tier (0 to 10).

### 5.2 Raids
* Introduction raid first (always ordinary and gentle), then raids on a sparse night schedule; the player can pick a lower level, call early, or pay to push back.
* Waves: **3 + tier (capped at 5 extra)**. Every third raid is a **boss raid** (the boss arrives in the last wave; 50 percent larger cache, bonus kits and grenades).
* **Attackers (1.1.4):** each wave's count is `Rules.boosted`: 15% more, rounded up, at least one more, at most `Rules.WAVE_CAP` (166); Reinforcements then multiply it by 1.3 (`RaidModifiers.attackers`).
* **Elites and modifiers (1.1.4):** see Part B (Elite mobs, Raid modifiers). Elites per wave `Elites.count`; the raid's attribute balancing keeps the mod's own elite and modifier changes (`Elites.reset` keeps the fixed modifier ids); the client draws a Badass larger from the size sent with the raid's marks (`RaidTypes.Marks`; an elite made by the operator command outside a raid is sent too). 1.1.5: Badass sizes `Elites.BADASS_SIZES` (1.5, 2.2, 3, 4, 5), step `Elites.stride` (0.6 x (size - 1) on top of the mob's own, `STEP_HEIGHT_ADDITION`, kept by the balancing); a giant (`Elites.GIANT`, size 2+) that is idle in the raid loop calls `RaidBreaching.smash` (its hitbox moved one block toward its target, every breakable block in it from its feet up, at most `SMASH_LIMIT` 24; inside the zone only snapshot blocks, through the damage journal). 1.1.6: a Badass loses `Elites.slowdown(level, creeper)` of its speed (MULTIPLY_TOTAL on the `SPEED` id, which a Badass never needs for Speedster), and a Badass creeper's `explosionRadius` is set to `Elites.blast(radius, size)` when it is made. Fog of War: `EternalDefense.revealed` is false, so no raider gets the glowing tag, at spawn or after. 1.1.8: the client scales the pose around `EntityRenderer.render` inside `EntityRenderDispatcher.render` (`EliteScaleMixin`, so GeckoLib renderers too) and undoes it for the name tag, which it lifts to 0.5 x size over the hitbox (bar 0.56 x size, mark 0.8 x size: `EliteClient.lift`); `EliteClient.overlay` (`RenderLevelStageEvent` AFTER_ENTITIES, mobs in view within 96 blocks) draws the raid mark, the parachute, and for an elite its ring (`RenderType.eyes`, radius max(0.7, width x 0.85)) and health bar (width clamp(width x 0.8, 0.9, 2.4)); `RaidTypes.Marks` sends each elite's code (`Elites.code`: kind x 8 + level).
* Attackers scale with the number of defenders, spawn outside the zone, target doors first and dig through walls; ranged enemies hold distance.
* **Gate spawns and the speed burst (0.0.20):** a reinforcement looks for natural ground first (the rules below). When none has been found for `RaidSpawns.GATE_AFTER_TICKS` (200, 10 s), it comes out of a red gate (`RaiderGates.spawnGate`, the exit-gate sprite, with the teleport particles and sound) on the nearest ground that will do: rings from the outer edge of the active chunks in to `GATE_BEYOND_ZONE` (12) blocks past the zone's edge, outdoors (sky light 13 or more), standing room on solid ground nobody built, no fluid, within `GATE_HEIGHT` (32) of the beacon's level and at least `GATE_PLAYER_DISTANCE` (12) from every player. The gate stands `RaiderGates.SPAWN_GATE_BEHIND` (1.2) blocks behind the enemy, seen from where it is heading (the beacon for a raid, the nearest player for a warband), and is drawn 1.6 times the size of a channel gate (1.6 x 3.2 blocks), so the enemy is seen stepping out of it instead of hiding it; enemies that come out close together share a gate; it closes 5 s after the last one. Chat says once a wave either "coming out of red gates closer in" or, with no ground at all, "stay near the beacon so the land stays loaded" (the old line, repeated every 30 s, is gone). Every raid enemy (ordinary, Special Forces, the boss) starts with Speed II for `RaidSpawns.RUSH_SECONDS` (10). **Rule (owner, 0.0.20):** every enemy the mod places anywhere but a natural spawn comes out of a red gate. `/eternaldefense test-gate` (operators) opens one in front of the player with a zombie.
* **Night only and the interval (0.0.21):** a raid that is due (`preparationTicks` at least `Rules.intervalDays(t)` x 24000: max(3, 10 - t) active days for reward tier t) starts only while the day time is in `Rules.RAID_HOUR_FROM` 13000 to `RAID_HOUR_UNTIL` 21000 and a defender is near the beacon. Start raid by day sets `CampaignData.raidRequested` (saved, synced as `raidRequested`): the raid starts at the next raid hour with a defender near; a booked raid ends bought break time as starting one did; `begin` clears the booking. The introduction raid starts at once, whatever the time.
* **Armed raiders (0.0.21):** raid mobs are made without the vanilla spawn equipment, so a raid skeleton had no bow and a pillager no crossbow: they got the ranged beacon goal, could not shoot, walked like zombies and were sent through gate after gate as stuck. `RaidBalance.arm` gives a skeleton (any `AbstractSkeleton`) a bow and a pillager a crossbow before the mob joins the level, and again in the balance pass; `BeaconCombat.attach` gives the ranged beacon attack only to a skeleton or pillager holding its weapon, the melee one otherwise.
* **Ordinary monsters during a raid (0.0.21):** between raids a wild hostile mob is drawn to the beacon when it is within 24 blocks and sees it; during a raid (`Objective.raid`) cover no longer hides it and every hostile mob within the zone's radius + `BeaconCombat.RAID_ATTRACT_BEYOND` (48) blocks of the beacon comes for it.
* **Marching (v3):** raiders walk **straight at the beacon** (no detours around terrain) and only stop to dig when they stop making progress (less than a block in a second). They spawn on **open surface ground at or above the beacon's level** (never in a crevice or below ground; if no such place exists for 30 s the rule relaxes by 4 blocks, after 90 s by 10). Every raider carries a **red exclamation mark** above its head and is **immune to sunlight**.
* **Sunlight protection (v4):** prevents daylight ignition only. Flame arrows, lava and fire traps still deal damage; native fire-immune species retain their own immunity.
* **Raider Gates (0.0.14, replaces the silent rescue of 0.0.13):** `RaidRescue.tick` looks at every ground raider about once a second. Two triggers start a channel. (a) *Look-ahead probe* (`RaidSpawns.lookAhead`): `LOOKAHEAD_BLOCKS` (10) samples, one per block, along the raider's straight line to the beacon, read from the `MOTION_BLOCKING_NO_LEAVES` height map without loading a chunk; it triggers on lava or magma, on a drop of `GATE_DROP_BLOCKS` (6) between neighbouring samples, or on `GATE_FLUID_SPAN` (8) wet samples in a row (a shorter pond is waded). Unloaded columns, tree trunks, player-placed blocks and everything inside the zone are *unknown*: never blocked, and they break a run of water. (b) *Progress timer* (`RaidMarch.stuck`): no `PROGRESS_BLOCKS` (3) gain in the 3D distance to the beacon for `STUCK_SECONDS` (25) while not busy. Busy means: within melee reach of the beacon with line of sight; a ranged raider within `FIRING_DISTANCE` (14) with line of sight; it broke or damaged a block in the last `BREACH_BUSY_SECONDS` (5); a player within `ENGAGED_RADIUS` (12) while something hurt the raider in the last 5 seconds; or its chunk is not entity-ticking. Busy time restarts the 25-second clock. Both triggers wait out the 10-second grace window after a gate. A raider that triggers either one asks `RaidSpawns.findRescue` for a destination and opens a gate (`RaiderGates.open`).
  * *Destination.* On the staging ring at the zone radius plus `CLEARANCE` (64) plus the usual spread (up to 16); every spawn rule (`safe`: outside the zone, entity-ticking chunk, inside the world border, at the beacon's level, open level ground) applies; the whole straight way from there to the zone edge passes the same probe (`RaidSpawns.lineProbe`, one sample every `DESTINATION_SPACING` (2) blocks); at least 24 blocks from every player (checked again when the destination is shown and when the raider appears); another sector than the one the raider was stuck in when any other will do. Never inside the zone.
  * *Channel.* `RaiderGates`: the raider stands next to a `RaiderGate` (1.6 blocks to its side) for `CHANNEL_SECONDS` (8). It cannot move: `ChannelGoal`, priority -1 with MOVE, LOOK and JUMP flags, holds it (it does not use `setNoAi`, which belongs to defeated Special Forces soldiers); a ring of red dust particles shows on it every second (the vanilla particle packet). Every second in which it lost health delays the end by `DAMAGE_DELAY_SECONDS` (3), at most `MAX_DELAY_SECONDS` (10) in total, so a tough raider under long-range fire cannot stall the wave into the raid timeout; death cancels the channel. `TELEGRAPH_SECONDS` (3) before the end the destination shows portal particles and a portal sound. On arrival: navigation stopped, fall distance and motion reset, the tracker reset with the 10-second grace window.
  * *One gate per obstacle.* A raider that gets stuck within `GATE_JOIN_RADIUS` (8) of a live gate uses it; a gate has room for `GATE_CAPACITY` (8) channelers, the rest wait for a free place. The gate (`RaiderGate`, an entity that is never saved, cannot be entered, hit, pushed or targeted, drawn with the Return portal sprite in red at about half its speed so it cannot be taken for a player's Return portal) goes away when no raider is assigned to it, and when a raid starts or ends, the campaign is packed up or the server stops. Nothing is persisted: after a reload the raiders fall back to the progress timer.
  * *Exit gate (0.0.16).* When the destination is shown (3 s before the jump) a second `RaiderGate` opens on the destination block; nobody joins an exit gate. If the destination has to change at the last check, the exit moves with it. It closes `EXIT_LINGER_SECONDS` (2) after the raider stepped out, at once if the channel is cancelled, and when its raider is gone. Not saved, like the gate.
  * *Escalation.* The gate count is kept on the mob (`eternalGates`, so it survives chunk reloads). After `MAX_GATES` (3) the next trigger withdraws the raider: discarded, removed from the raid, no drop, no Ardent Energy; one chat line per wave ("N stuck raiders withdrew"). If no destination exists the attempt is retried after the grace window and an ordinary raider is withdrawn after `DRY_LIMIT` (3) failed attempts. A raid boss (`eternalBoss`) gets another gate every time and is never withdrawn (that would fail the raid with "Boss was not defeated"); each is logged.
  * *Not covered, on purpose.* Flyers and gliders (vex, phantom, blaze), paratroopers while their parachute is on, Special Forces soldiers; siege-creeper ignition is unchanged. Raiders still march straight: no detour pathfinding and `BeaconCombat.approach` is unchanged (it only records when its forced-move fallback ran, for the log).
* **Marchable corridors (0.0.13):** the staging ring is cut into 12 world-aligned wedges of 30 degrees (wedge 0 starts due east). Once per raid, the first time a wedge is needed, its centre ray is sampled every 4 blocks from 8 blocks beyond the zone to the farthest staging ring, using the heights of the `MOTION_BLOCKING_NO_LEAVES` map and no chunk loading. A surface of water, lava or magma, a rise of more than 2 blocks or a drop of more than 3 between neighbouring samples is a violation; a sample in an unloaded chunk, or one whose top block is a tree trunk or a player's block, is unknown and never a violation. A spawn at distance D is judged by the samples between D and the zone. `RaidSpawns.find` tries the same rings and 12 candidates per ring as before (random wedge to start, random angle inside it), but candidates in clean wedges come first and then the fewest violations; every candidate is checked at most once. Heuristic: the cached ray is the wedge's centre, a candidate's real line can differ by up to 15 degrees. Diagnostics: `-Deternaldefense.stuckLog=true` logs every stuck, rescue and withdraw as one `[stuck] event=... key=value` line (mob type, role, short id, wave, position, distance, blocks at feet, below and ahead, fluid, biome, rescue number, destination, and whether `approach()` had to push the mob straight at its goal because it found no path).
* **Trap adaptation (v4):** each class first causing positive trap damage gets one full-damage wave; subsequent waves gain 25/50/75/100% resistance to that class. Eleven classes cover fire, fall, drowning, suffocation, spikes, crushing/cramming, freezing, explosion, ownerless projectile, magic and lightning traps. Announcements mark each step. Learning is saved for the current raid and resets at the next raid. Attributed weapons, turrets and support cannon attacks are excluded; source-erasing addons need integration tags/markers. See `docs/RAID-ADAPTATION.md`.
* **Victory** restores everything the raid broke (damage journal). **Defeat** leaves it.
* Warnings: chat one day ahead, then every six hours; popups for raid start, each wave, VICTORY (confetti) and DEFEAT.

* **Defender deaths (0.0.41 display).** A player dying near the beacon during a raid counts (`CampaignData.deaths`, reset when a raid starts); every `Rules.DEATHS_PER_PENALTY` (3) deaths the beacon loses a tenth of its maximum health (`Rules.deathPenalty`). The HUD's health line and the Overview's show a skull and `Rules.livesLeft` = 3 - deaths mod 3 (3, 2, 1, red at 1), sent as `livesLeft` in the beacon status.

### 5.3 Special raid types (chosen in Raid control since 1.1.4; before, from the fourth raid on, 50 percent chance)
| Type | What it asks of the base |
| --- | --- |
| **Air raid** | Vexes, phantoms, blazes ignore walls; build anti-air, not thicker walls. Vexes and phantoms are steered straight at the closest defender within 7 blocks, otherwise at the beacon, and hit it on arrival; blazes keep their own ranged attack |
| **Paratroopers** | Heavies at half count drop from 46 blocks up under red parachutes (slow fall, can be shot, no fall damage if not shot); every one of them wears the parachute |
| **Siege** | Ranged-heavy waves; creepers (30 percent) dig into walls, shooters fire through the holes. A creeper only blows up when it is stuck at a wall within 24 blocks of the zone (or reaches the beacon), never where it spawns |
| **They are thousands** | Only zombies and husks, double count, spawning every 8 ticks instead of 20, up to 96 alive; no heavy or ranged |

A won special raid adds **ceil((3 + 2 x tier) x 1.25)** Ardent Energy and raises the drop chance of its mobs by 15%. The next raid is ordinary unless the team picks a type on the Raid modifiers page; the choice is announced and shown in the Overview, and cleared when the raid ends. Design intent: a type that rewards a counter-build (anti-air, thick walls, killing zones).

### 5.4 Ardent Energy and the Exchange
* **Drops (1.1.4):** `ArdentEnergy.drop`: a mob without the red mark drops one only when it is a boss (tag, raid boss, or 150+ default health) with **300+ health**, else nothing. A red-marked mob (`eternalRaider` or a Tactical Operation warband) rolls `baseDropChance` (6.67%) x (`markedDropMultiplier` (1.5, 1.1.5) + the raid's drop percent / 100) x (1 + 0.25 x Luck level) (`ArdentEnergy.multiplier`; all six modifiers and a special raid add 50, so 2.0), +1 percent per Looting level; 60+ health drops two; a raid boss always drops `bossDrop`; an elite adds its own (`Elites.reward`). A marked mob drops without a player's kill too. Config in `eternal-defense-common.toml`: `baseDropChance`, `markedDropMultiplier`, `lootingBonusPerLevel`, `bossDrop`.
* **Raid income (1.1.4):** a guaranteed payout per won raid of **ceil((1 + t) x 1.25)**, raised with the rest of the cache by the modifiers' payout percent, plus 1 for each wave that had an elite, plus drops, plus the special raid bonus.
* **Exchange Shop** (placed inside the zone; 3D vending booth): **Sell** tab (hand in materials, receive energy) and **Buy** tab (pay energy, receive goods). Buying always costs more than selling so loops never pay (a unit test checks the defaults). Offers live in `config/eternal-defense-exchange.txt` (`[sell]`, `[buy]`). Defaults since 0.0.21: both editions buy `minecraft:iron_ingot x16 = 1` and `minecraft:iron_block x16 = 8` (144 ingots for 8 energy instead of 9); the pack edition also buys `minecraft:gold_ingot x8 = 2`, `minecraft:blaze_rod = 3` (**6** since 0.0.41, owner; the untouched 0.0.28 default file is replaced, `ExchangeShop.PACK_0028`), `minecraft:ender_pearl = 3` and `minecraft:diamond = 6`, the standalone prices. A file that is still exactly the 0.0.20 default of its edition is replaced by the new default the next time the shop opens (`ExchangeShop.oldDefault`); an edited file is kept. **0.0.28 (owner):** both editions buy the Warlord Seal (`eternal_defense:warlord_seal = 4` pack, `= 8` standalone); an untouched 0.0.21 to 0.0.27 default is replaced the same way, an edited file needs the line added by hand. **0.0.43 (owner):** `minecraft:iron_ore x16 = 2` replaces `minecraft:iron_ingot x16 = 1`, `minecraft:iron_block x8 = 8` replaces `x16 = 8`, `create:andesite_alloy x16 = 1` replaces `= 2`; the untouched 0.0.41/0.0.42 default (`ExchangeShop.PACK_0041`) is replaced, an edited file is kept. **1.0.0:** no file is converted any more (`oldDefault` and the `PACK_00xx` lists are gone): the default is written when the file is missing, and an existing file is used as it is.
* Late-game factory players sell surplus for ammunition; players without a factory farm energy and buy parts at a premium.

### 5.5 Support system (per player)
Each player owns **one Support Platform** and **one Support Cannon**, placed anywhere inside the beacon zone, bought at the beacon’s Workshop Fabrication tab; relocated for free with the Beacon Recovery Shovel (keeps upgrades and contents). Only the owner can use them. They stop working if the beacon is gone.
* **Support Platform** Mk-1 to Mk-4 (grids 3x3 to 6x6; upgrade plating 16, 32, 64). Two blocks tall, owner sign on a post above it (render-only, no hitbox). The two blocks in front are a **no-build return zone** ("KEEP RETURN ZONE CLEAR").
* **Support Cannon** (3x3, heavy; brass owner plate). It turns with a looping turning sound, locks on and fires when the lock sound ends (0.0.18).
* **Flares** (throwing is free; buying costs energy): **Supply** 3, **Return** 2, **Fire Support** 6, **Loadout** 5 (1.1.0, see 5.13); cannon purchase 12.
  * *Supply Flare:* the cannon fires and a supply chest parachutes in with the platform's contents (12 s outside, 7 s underground, counted from the shot).
  * *Return Flare:* the cannon fires it and a portal opens (30 s; 5 min and usable twice with the upgrade). It needs the Support Cannon like every flare (0.0.26; it was spared from round 2 to 0.0.25); the return gate an operation opens when there is no ground needs none. The player arrives at the front cell, a nearby safe cell, or the return is cancelled and the flare not consumed.
  * *Fire Support Flare:* a red box marks the target; the cannon shells it. A second flare mid-barrage waits ("Barrage in process").
* **Twelve fire support types** (the owner's choice applies even to a borrowed flare; `CannonUpgrades.FireType`, saved and sent by ordinal, so new types are only ever appended): Explosion Barrage (6 shells), Spotter Arrows (6 volleys; the Arrow Cluster of tipped arrows until 0.0.36, see below), Narukami's Favor (lightning, 5), Bunker Buster (one bomb digging an 8-block-half-width cube; every hostile mob in it takes five shells' damage, `CannonUpgrades.BUNKER_HITS`, 125 before the Damage upgrade, 0.0.28, was two; hurts players for half their health; refuses inside the zone; spares obsidian, crying obsidian, bedrock and anything with resistance 1,200 or more), Healing Barrage, Curse of Debilitation (see below), and (0.0.16, reworked in 0.0.18; damage is a share of the 25-damage shell, times the Damage upgrade):

### 5.8 Mess Hall and prepared meals
**A fortress should have a kitchen feeding the war effort.** A farmer/cook supports the group with portable expedition rations and communal pre-raid meals. Casual cooking is useful; a dedicated cook supports more people without being mandatory.

* **Mess Hall Mk I–IV:** a rotating **2 wide × 1 deep × 2 tall** installation (painted models from the artist package), entirely inside the existing beacon zone, with up to six shared persistent mixing slots (three open at Mk I to III, all six at Mk IV), a physical sandwich output and a bread-only base slot.
* **What each level adds (0.0.11):** Mk I: sandwiches only, meals last **15** minutes, **3** food slots. Mk II: unlocks **stew**, **20** minutes. Mk III: **25** minutes, unlocks **×2 doubling**. Mk IV: **30** minutes, **6** food slots. Pots (1/2/3/4) are unchanged; stew batch units are 12/21/30/36 since 0.0.35 (4/7/10/12 before); stew servings per pot are 3/5/7/9 since 0.0.14 (they were 4/8/12/16). The screen shows the current level's meal length, food slots and ×2 status as three chips, and the Upgrade button's tooltip says what the next level adds. Each tier has distinct functional model resources: improvised wooden spit, copper field station, stone fortified ovens, enclosed iron/green canteen. Initial craft uses vanilla materials in both editions; paid in-place upgrades preserve food and links.
* **Cook Pot:** **1 × 1 × 2**, hollow pot with a conditional stew surface and, on a chalkboard in its upper block, a live menu board displaying stew, up to three bonuses and remaining servings. State, strengths, remaining servings and hall identity persist in its block entity.
* **Two preparation tabs:** Sandwich produces one portable item with **1–2 effects**, stored in NBT, stacking to 16 and edible normally at full hunger. Stew holds **up to 3 effects**, assigned to one selected empty linked pot. Sandwich consumes two base bread plus two of each of two or three distinct fillings (0.0.35); base bread contributes no Grain or effect. Stew consumes **12/21/30/36 ingredient units** (0.0.35; 4/7/10/12 before) at Mk I/II/III/IV, spread across stacked inputs, including one of every composition type. A hall holds as many kinds of food as it has open food slots (3, or 6 at Mk IV). Container remainders return for every consumed item. Failed preparation consumes nothing, and a pot that still has stew is only replaced after the cook confirms it.
* **Bowl serving:** interact with a filled pot while holding a normal bowl to eat immediately. The server removes one serving; the bowl is used up (0.0.37; it stayed reusable before). Sandwich nutrition is 8 (saturation modifier 0.6); stew nutrition is 10 (0.8).
* **Tier hooks:** Mk I/II/III/IV link **1/2/3/4 pots** and produce **3/5/7/9 stew servings** (lowered from 4/8/12/16 in 0.0.14). All tiers retain the same effect-slot limits. Next upgrades cost **8/16/32 Reinforced Plating** in the pack or **4/8/14 Ardent Energy** standalone (`Economy`). Creative upgrades are free.
* **Composition:** six slots count distinct item types once; at most two sandwich or three stew effects. Vanilla ingredients give only ordinary Minecraft bonuses. Ordinary is the default. Players click recipe icons on the kitchen screen to choose effects; **Gather ingredients** then fetches the foods from the pack and **Take back** returns them. The order and mode are stored with the hall. Fifteen selected legendary recipes cover pairs of six food groups and replace those groups’ ordinary bonuses; selected recipes cannot overlap groups. Every ordinary effect has a vanilla ×2 pair, allowing three selected ordinary effects at Mk II or III (one food each) and all of them doubled at Mk IV (six slots). Weights rank ordinary effects without extra strength tiers. **Doubling unlocks at Mk III, in sandwiches and in stew**: two distinct foods in one group contributing the same ordinary effect. A legendary effect needs two from EACH of its groups (four slots), so only Mk IV can double one. Split stacks never qualify. Exact matrix: `MESS-HALL-V6-IMPLEMENTATION.md`.
* **Field bonuses:** ordinary health +4, armor +2, knockback resistance +5%, slower hunger 15% (Hardy, cap 60%), melee damage/speed +8%, luck +1, healing 1 health/5 s, explosion protection 20% (Blast Ward, cap 80%), arrow/bolt/fireball/trident protection 15% (Projectile Ward, cap 90%, never TaCZ bullets), swim +10%. Legendary Firepower +8%, Quick Hands +10%, Brawler +20%, Heavy Hand +15%, Demolition +10%; new Recoil Control −10%, Focus +10% accuracy, Snap Aim +10%, Fast Draw +10%, Dead Eye +8%, Gun Mobility +8%, Impact +0.25 knockback, Rapid Fire +5%, Scoped/Hip Focus +15%. Backend TaCZ Attributes 1.4 remains required. Owned transient UUID modifiers preserve other mods; recoil uses a negative multiplier, Impact adds to neutral zero, Focus updates aimed and hip-fire accuracy. Native bash/radius/reload adapters retain v4 behavior.
* **Home enhancement:** `BaseZone` determines valid home territory. Meal strength is **2× at home**, and remaining field duration **freezes**. Outside, strength normalizes and the timer resumes. Returning home freezes the existing remainder and never refreshes it. New meals replace the old state and start the length of the hall that made them: **15 / 20 / 25 / 30 minutes** (18,000 / 24,000 / 30,000 / 36,000 field ticks) at Mk I / II / III / IV. The length is saved on the sandwich, the stew and the active meal.
* **Lifecycle:** server-owned UUID-keyed SavedData persists composition and exact ticks. Logout/restart pause time. Dimension changes preserve the meal; other dimensions count as field. Non-death respawn retains it. **Death clears meals and their owned modifiers.** A meal is shown with ordinary vanilla potion effects (no custom HUD): one effect per bonus with its current value and time in the inventory list, endless while at home, plus a **Home Zone** effect ("your food buffs double and don't decay"). The kitchen shows available/required food units and per-slot consumption tooltips; preview tooltips list field/home effects.
* **Linking:** nearby anchors link within **8 blocks in 3D**, respecting tier capacity. Existing valid links are not stolen. Hall UUID + persisted pot reservations prevent same-position replacement and chunk-unload reassignment. Unloaded pots retain reserved capacity. Broken anchors clean loaded links; unloaded partners validate later. Orphaned stew remains stored until the pot reconnects. No general logistics system exists.

* **MRE Station (0.0.38):** see Part F3. MRE: nutrition the sum (at most 20), saturation the sum of nutrition x modifier x 2 per food (at most 20), no effects, 16 ticks, only when hungry, stacks to 64 by equal totals. Each MRE: one of each food in six slots, 1 Marines Fuel (clay ball + any dye -> 2), 1 Paper.

Balance is an initial estimate. Extended co-op balance and full-pack integration remain playtest work. Exact extension format, lifecycle and ownership rules: `docs/MESS-HALL.md`.

### 5.9 Tactical Operations (0.0.20)
Truth on the server only, in the SavedData `eternal_defense_hunts_v1` (`HuntData`): the board, the one mission, the table and satellite positions, the satellite's Mk, the scan images. Item NBT is never trusted (a map or flare only points at the mission; the satellite's Mk is kept in `HuntData`, not on the item). Overworld only. Nothing loads or generates a chunk except one bounded structure lookup per Garrison offer when it is made.
* **Blocks.** `command_table` (anchor plus 7 invisible structure parts, facing the player) and `satellite_beacon` (`mk` 1..3 block state, mirrored from `HuntData.satelliteMk`). Placement through `BaseZone.ZoneItem` (same refusals as all support gear; "one per base"). Prices (`Economy.fabrication`): table 24 iron, 16 copper, 16 redstone, 8 glass and 4 reinforced plating (pack) or 6 Ardent Energy (standalone); satellite 16 iron, 24 copper, 8 redstone, 4 gold and 2 resonance coils (pack) or 4 Ardent Energy (standalone). Satellite upgrades (`Economy.satellite`): Mk II 16 coils or 10 Ardent Energy, Mk III 32 coils or 20 Ardent Energy, paid at the table (the server checks the price and that the player stands within 8 blocks).
* **Uplink.** Satellite within `TacticalRules.UPLINK_DISTANCE` (8, straight line) of the table, both in the zone, the dish's sky light 15. Reasons shown: no beacon, no table, no satellite, zone, too far, no sky.
* **Board.** Offers by Mk: 2 / 3 / 4; range 256 / 512 / 1024 blocks from the beacon. Filled on the first uplink (and the new slots after an upgrade, `HuntBoard.toFill`); afterwards one new offer at the first tick of a new day when short (`dawnOffers`). Placement: a random bearing at least 40 degrees from every other offer, a distance from the zone radius + 150 to the range, inside the world border (64 tries), never on water (0.0.21, `Hunts.dryLand`: the generator's biome at sea level, read without loading, at the spot and 12 blocks (`DRY_MARGIN`) north, south, east and west, is no ocean, deep ocean or river; an offer made before 0.0.21 that lies on water is replaced, one every 1200 ticks). Garrison: whitelisted vanilla structures (pillager outpost, desert pyramid, jungle temple, swamp hut, igloo, woodland mansion) from each structure set's random spread, filtered by the same window and by the generator's biome at the spot (read without loading), at most 4 presence checks per lookup and one lookup per server tick (a second Garrison rolled while a board fills waits for the next pass, a second later) (`StructureManager.checkStructurePresence`: a structure counts when the chunk's saved data has it, or, for a chunk never generated, when vanilla's simulation of the generator says it starts there), the time logged (a warning above 50 ms); none found: a Patrol. Codenames: two 20-word lists, deterministic from the world seed and the offer id.
* **Accepting.** Refused with NO UPLINK, during a raid (RAID IN PROGRESS), within `WARNING_TICKS` (24000, one day) of the next raid (RAID WARNING), or while a mission runs. A running mission is never cancelled by a raid; taking the beacon down cancels it and removes its warband.
* **Map and flare.** On accept every online player gets a vanilla filled map with a red X, centred halfway between the beacon and the objective (made with that centre, not on vanilla's map grid, on which a beacon near a grid line falls off the sheet), at the smallest scale (0 to 4) that holds both 16 blocks in from the edge (`TacticalRules.mapCentre`, `mapScale`), named with the codename and tagged `eternalMission`; and one Return Flare per player per offer (an offer abandoned and taken again gives no second flare). A player logging in mid-mission gets what is missing; the table reprints a lost map (no flare). A full inventory drops the item at the player.
* **Map burning (0.0.21).** A mission map whose mission is not running and whose offer is not on the board burns (removed from the inventory and the off hand, with flames, a hiss and one chat line; a map left in a chest burns when picked up): at once when the mission is cleared or found unreachable, at login, and every 100 ticks for everyone online (`Hunts.burnMaps`, `mapStale`). An abandoned offer goes back on the board, so its map stays.
* **Warband** (`HuntWarband`). Patrol 8-12, Garrison 14-20, Warlord 10-14 escorts plus a captain; +2 per extra online player, at most +8. Roles, types and stats from `RaidBalance` at the current reward tier; the captain is a heavy with twice the health, glowing, named with the codename (no boss bar). **Arrival (0.0.21, replaces the 240-try spot search):** when a player is within 64 blocks and the objective's chunk is entity-ticking, the first column from the X outwards (a disc of `TacticalRules.ANCHOR_SEARCH` 16 blocks, nearest first, loaded columns only) whose top is a sturdy dry block that is no log, leaves, magma, cactus or powder snow, with two free blocks above, is the **anchor**. A small zone (`MINI_ZONE`, 6 blocks) is drawn around it with end-rod light like the beacon's. One mob a tick comes out of a red gate on the ring `RING_FROM` 8 to `RING_TO` 12 blocks from the anchor (6 random tries for a standable column within 8 blocks of the anchor's height and 3 blocks from players; else the anchor itself: one block is all a warband needs) and rushes to the anchor at speed 1.2 (`RushAnchor`, unless it is fighting within 12 blocks). The disc fully loaded and no block found: every player within 64 blocks gets a return gate home (`Hunts.returnGate`, the Return Flare's portal, owner only, open `RETURN_GATE_TICKS` 6000, 5 minutes; no support cannon needed), the maps burn and the offer is replaced at once. The live mobs are sent with the raid's marks (`RaidTypes.Marks`), so they carry the red exclamation mark. Every mob is persistent, leashed (`restrictTo`) 32 blocks around the anchor, sun-proof, tagged `eternalHunt` (mission id) and `eternalHuntSerial`, never a raider, never sent at the beacon, drops no random Ardent Energy. A mob loaded from disk whose mission or serial is not the running one is discarded. Mobs lost without dying are spawned again after 10 s (never written off).
* **States.** OFFERED, ACTIVE (accepted), ENGAGED (the warband is out; total and remaining counted by deaths), CLEARED (set before anything is paid, only once). Nobody within 128 blocks for 6000 ticks while ENGAGED: the warband is discarded and the mission is ACTIVE again with a new serial. Operator commands: `/eternaldefense hunt status|reset|complete`.
* **Pay.** g(t) = 2 + 2t (standalone) or 1 + t (pack), t the beacon's *Next payout* tier up to 10 (`Hunts.tier`, the same `BeaconNetwork.prospectiveTier` the Overview shows; 0.0.22: it was the tier of the last raid won, so a campaign that had won only its first raid paid tier 0 whatever its base, the owner's "0 Ardent Energy and 1 coin"); the warband's roles and stats and the danger pips use the same tier; budget B = k x g with k = 0.5 (Patrol), 1.0 (Garrison), 1.75 (Warlord); Ardent Energy round(0.6 B) and Universal Ammo Coins round(0.4 B x 16 / 3), the Exchange's coin price, into the reward chest (`RaidRewards.queue`). Coins never sell back for Ardent Energy at the Exchange. At tier 3: standalone 4 + 18, 5 + 17, 8 + 30; pack 2 + 8, 2 + 9, 4 + 15 (the Patrol doubled, below). A cleared Warlord also pays one Warlord Seal (0.0.27). **Minimum (0.0.22, owner):** never less than `Economy.HUNT_MIN_ARDENT` (1) Ardent Energy and `HUNT_MIN_COINS` (2) coins, whatever the tier (only a pack Patrol at tier 0, which paid 0 + 1, is raised). **Patrol (0.0.28, owner):** a Patrol pays twice the formula, the minimum included (`Economy.PATROL_PAY_FACTOR`), at every tier: some bases are offered little but Patrols (a Satellite Beacon below Mk III offers only Patrols and Garrisons, and a Garrison with no whitelisted structure in range becomes a Patrol). A Patrol now pays about what a Garrison does.
* **Scan** (`TacticalScan`, `ScanSampler`). Two 256 x 256 images centred on the beacon: Base (one block a pixel) and Region (the range, 2/4/8 blocks a pixel). Vanilla map colours and shading from loaded chunks only (`getChunkNow`; the first block with a map colour from the `WORLD_SURFACE` height, water shaded by depth); a pixel not loaded now keeps its last colour (stale, dimmed); one never seen shows its biome from the generator (hatched). 2 rows a tick, one scan at a time for the server, 30 s cooldown; saved deflated. The finished images go once per version to each viewer.
* **Network** (protocol 30 since 0.0.21, when `MissionSync` gained the mission id for the hand check; 29 in 0.0.20): S2C `MissionSync` (17, the status card line), `ScanImage` (18, the images through `ChunkedPayload`), `ScanProgress` (19, the sweep line), `TableState` (20, the open table refreshed in place; reopening would throw the cursor to the middle of the window). The board travels in the menu's opening buffer; the table's buttons are vanilla menu buttons (no new client-to-server packet).
* **Sounds.** Tactical accept, refuse, scan sweep, scan done and contact (`tools/audio/tactical.py`, near -17 dB mean); `music.mission_cleared` is a placeholder that plays the Victory Fanfare until the owner's song arrives.

### 5.10 Forward Operating Bases (0.0.23 to 0.0.27, slices 1 to 4)
Truth on the server only, in the SavedData `eternal_defense_fobs_v1` (`FobData`): one record per FOB Beacon (id, name, anchor, the section the village was found in, state PLANTED / ACTIVE / DOWN, whether it ever won a raid, the vanilla raid it follows and how many villagers lived there when that raid began; slices 2 and 3 fill a route and a list of dead villagers; 0.0.31 adds the tribute slots, choices and tallies, the specialty, the storage, the last zone's columns for the Command Table and the time of the last delivery, and moves a 0.0.30 stockpile's whole items into the storage). Shared by every player, linked by id and never by name. One broken record is skipped on load, never the whole list. `CampaignData` is never written (it is only asked whether the beacon is planted).
* **Block.** `fob_beacon`: an anchor plus one structure part above (the owner's model reaches y 32, `FobBlocks.TALL`; the flag's tip 0.2 px past the block), facing the player who planted it. Unbreakable (strength -1, blast resistance 3,600,000; withers and the dragon cannot break it or its upper cell, which cannot be mined either), never pushed; a blast that breaks the upper cell has it rebuilt a tick later. A creative break or a command that removes the block deletes the FOB. Price (`Economy.fabrication`, `FOB_*`): 16 iron, 16 copper, 8 redstone, 4 glass, and 2 logistics modules and 2 reinforced plating (pack) or 8 Ardent Energy (standalone).
* **Planting** (`Fobs.plantProblem`, then `FobRules.plantProblem` in this order): Overworld only; refused with no beacon planted, with `MAX_FOBS` (5) FOBs placed (any state), on Peaceful or with the `disableRaids` game rule on, outside a village, in a village that already has a FOB, with fewer than `MIN_VILLAGERS` (3) villagers in its zone, or with no room for both cells. The zone of a FOB (`FobZone`) is vanilla's village: every 16-block section within one section of a bed, workstation or bell **that a villager has claimed** (vanilla's occupied points of interest; `ServerLevel.sectionsToVillage`, `VILLAGE_SECTIONS` 1), joined to the FOB Beacon's section, at most `ZONE_REACH` (6) sections sideways and `ZONE_REACH_Y` (3) up or down, in loaded chunks only; worked out when needed, cached for 100 ticks, never saved, never loading a chunk. A generated name ("FOB IRON HOUND", from the hunt codename words, unique in any case) is given at planting.
* **Preview and outlines (0.0.30, `FobOutline`, `FobOutlineClient`).** Every 10 ticks, for a player in the Overworld holding a FOB Beacon (either hand), the server works out where the item would place it (the player's own reach and look, as `BlockPlaceContext` does), that spot's zone (`FobZone.sections`, kept 40 ticks per section looked at), its villagers, beds and workstations, and `Fobs.plantProblem` with that zone; every 40 ticks otherwise, the zones of the FOBs within `NEAR` (160) blocks whose outline is on (all of them while a FOB Beacon is held). A player is sent only what changed (and everything again every 200 ticks, on login and on a dimension change). The client draws each zone's outer walls (every side face of its 16-block sections with no zone section beyond it) in olive, with a slow moving grid every 4 blocks and a scan line, like the base zone's cyan one; the preview's zone in green (may be planted) or orange; the FOB's own model, see-through and tinted, at the spot the crosshair points at; and one line under the crosshair (villagers against the 3 needed, beds, workstations, or the refusal). Nothing is changed by the preview: the item checks again when used.
* **The raid.** The planting player gets Bad Omen I and `Raids.createOrExtendRaid` is called at once (vanilla's centre: the average of the occupied village points of interest within 64 blocks, else the player). The raid's id is kept and its status read once a second (Forge 1.20.1 has no raid event): victory makes the FOB ACTIVE and activated; vanilla's loss (the village's points of interest gone) or **fewer than half of the villagers counted at its start still alive** (`FobRules.raidFailed`, checked while the FOB is entity-ticking; the vanilla raid is then stopped) makes it DOWN; a raid that disappears or stops with no result leaves it as it was. Villagers belong to a FOB through a tag (`eternalFob`, the FOB's id), given to every villager in its zone and refreshed every 100 ticks (newcomers and children). Vanilla raiders are not tagged as the beacon's; near the home zone `BeaconCombat` still sends them at the beacon, as any hostile mob. They drop no Ardent Energy (`ArdentEnergy.vanillaRaider`: a `Raider` whose wave is above 0 with no `eternalRaider` tag).
* **Protection.** A villager tied to an existing FOB cannot be hurt (`LivingAttackEvent` cancelled at the highest priority, any source except those that bypass invulnerability: `/kill`, the void), except while that FOB's own raid runs; lightning does not turn one into a witch either (`EntityStruckByLightningEvent` cancelled the same way). ### 5.11 Logistics Center Hub and the supply source (0.0.40)
Truth on the server only, in the overworld's SavedData `eternal_defense_logistics_hubs` (`HubData`): one record per Hub (id, dimension, anchor, facing, placed, its 270 slots). The blocks only hold the Hub's id (`LogisticsHub.Cell`); the anchor is the hidden middle block and draws the model, the 26 parts are invisible.
* **Block and placement (`LogisticsHub`).** The item places the anchor one block above the clicked spot, facing the player; every cell must be in the world, loaded, replaceable, free of living entities, inside the Home Zone (`BaseZone.problem`) and outside a Return Zone; else refused with the zone, keep-clear or no-room message. Strength 5, blast resistance 3,600,000, not flammable, not pushable, mobs cannot break it; the anchor puts back a missing part every 2 seconds. Survival players cannot mine it while it holds items (no progress, action bar "Empty the Hub first"; the client knows from the anchor's synced flag); empty, breaking any block breaks the whole Hub and drops its item once. A creative break, or a removal by commands or other mods, drops the contents. The Recovery Shovel picks it up: the item keeps the Hub's id (and shows how many stacks wait), the items stay in the saved data, and placing that item again restores them.
* **Where it works.** Placed, in the Overworld, inside the Home Zone of a planted campaign; with the beacon's chunk loaded the beacon block must stand (as for all base gear), with it unloaded the campaign's record decides. Checked at most once a second (`SupplyRules.recheck`); when it stops working, the item handler is withdrawn (hoppers and Create let go) and right-click says why (outside / no beacon / not the Overworld).
* **Storage (`HubStorage`).** 270 slots; each slot at most 64 and at most the item's own stack size. An index is kept on every change: the count of each kind (item + tag), the slots of each kind, the empty slots. Insert fills stacks of the same kind first, then the first empty slots; take-outs come from the last slots of a kind. Per-slot handler calls (`getStackInSlot`, `insertItem`, `extractItem`, simulated or not) touch one slot and copy nothing; "how many X" reads the index.
* **Menu (`HubMenu`).** 6 rows of 9 (a page) over the player inventory, as the vanilla double chest. Pages 1 to 5 turn with the vanilla menu-button packet (buttons 0 and 1); the page and the slots in use travel as data slots. Valid while placed, working and within 10 blocks.
* **Automation.** `ForgeCapabilities.ITEM_HANDLER` on every face (and no face) of all 27 blocks: the Hub's one inventory. In the pack with Create loaded the parts are Create-aware (`CreateHubPart`, with Create's belt-input behaviour, inserting into the index), so a belt can end straight at the Hub; the mechanical arm has a `eternal_defense:logistics_hub` interaction point (`CreateArmCompat`). Both live in the pack jar only.
* **Supply source (`Supply`, `InventoryPayment`, `SupplyRules`).** A request is made at a spot (the station, the beacon, or the player for player-wide actions); the Hubs may pay when that spot is in the Home Zone (`Supply.at`). Sources: every pack slot (main and off hand, tier 0), then every kind each working Hub holds (tier 1 = nearest to the spot, ties by id). `SupplyRules.allocate` is a minimum-cost maximum flow: maximum paid first, then the fewest Hub items, then the nearest Hubs. Nothing is taken unless every cost is paid in full and the output fits; then the pack is written and the Hubs are drawn from. Gather moves (Mess Hall, Drink Station) borrow the Hubs' items as stand-ins after the table and the pack and take what was used. FOB actions (`Supply.pack`) never use a Hub. Creative players pay nothing.
* **The Hub line (`HubNotices`).** Only for the part a Hub paid: "Dev just pulled 54 Iron Ingot, 3 Oak Sign from the Hub", "Dev just spent 12 Ardent Energy from the Hub", or both in one line. The first three kinds are named (in the request's order), then "and N more". A player's pulls add up into one running total until 40 server ticks pass with no new pull (`SupplyRules.MERGE_TICKS`), then the line goes to the player and every player inside the Home Zone, and to the server log (`[Logistics Hub] ...`). Hoppers and Create never go through the supply source, so they send nothing.
* **Price (`Economy.HUB_*`, proposed).** 64 Reinforced Beacon Plating (pack) or 40 Ardent Energy (standalone), plus 64 iron ingots, 16 chests and 8 hoppers. For comparison: the next dearest installation is the Support Cannon (12 Ardent Energy); a Mess Hall from Mk I to Mk IV costs 60 plating or 30 Ardent Energy in all.

### 5.12 Factory parts and factory tiers (1.0.1; in the pack's scripts before)

### 5.13 Loadout Station, Loadout Coin, Loadout Flare and capsule (1.1.0)
The art pack's README (`docs/art/Eternal-Defense-Loadout/README.txt`) is the authority for every coordinate; the mod keeps those numbers in one class, `LoadoutModel` (each with the README line it comes from), and the rules in `LoadoutRules`.
* **Station block.** 3 x 3 x 3, the anchor in the middle (draws the model; no collision, no outline), 26 invisible cells pointing at it. Placed with the bottom-centre cell on the targeted spot, facing the player; every cell free, inside the Home Zone and outside a Return Zone. The four facings turn the model about its centre (8, 8, 8 px). Collision is the model's: the bottom-centre cell is a 2.5 px floor, the top-centre cell is solid from 5.5 px up, the other cells hold the body and the closed doors (solid for everybody but the player being printed, who has no collision with the station at all, so the walk in never pushes or suffocates them). Standing point: anchor + (0.5, -0.84375, 0.3125) blocks, facing out of the doors. Strength 5, blast resistance 3,600,000, not flammable, light 7 (the glowing panels are drawn full bright); survival players cannot mine it while any coin slot holds a coin; creative players can (the coins and the Locker drop). Saved by machine id in the overworld's SavedData `eternal_defense_loadout` (coins, remembered kinds, Locker, and each player's active slot and cooldown).
* **Price (`Economy.LOADOUT_*`, proposed, for the owner to adjust).** Workshop Fabrication: 64 Reinforced Beacon Plating, 8 Restoration Matrices, 32 iron ingots, 16 glass. The flagship of the list (the Hub is 64 plating, 64 iron, 16 chests, 8 hoppers).
* **Loadout Coin.** Make coin takes the 14 stacks in the pack area (armor feet to head, off hand, hotbar 1 to 9) exactly as they are (every tag: a TaCZ gun keeps its attachments, rounds and name) plus a fee of **4 Reinforced Beacon Plating and 1 Ardent Energy** (`Economy.COIN_FEE_*`), from the pack first, then the Home Zone's Hubs (with the Hub chat line). All or nothing. Refused: an empty pack area, a Loadout Coin inside, a shulker box or any item that carries items (a filled item inventory), and a kit over the size limit (`kitLimitKilobytes`, default 64 KB of NBT). The coin is sealed (it cannot be opened back into items except by printing), belongs to nobody (it can be traded), and stacks to 16 only with identical coins. Tooltip: the number of armor pieces, off hand yes/no, hotbar items, the first three names.
* **Coin slots and the active slot.** 9 slots shared by the base; a slot remembers the kind of the first coin put in and takes only identical coins until it is empty and forgotten (Forget). Each player's active slot (default slot 1) is stored by UUID.
* **Print (`LoadoutPrint`).** From the menu (the selected slot) or by sneaking and right-clicking the station (the active slot; an item in hand does not stop it), inside the Home Zone. Refused before anything changes unless: the station is free, the player stands in front of the doors within 4 blocks of the door's centre with a clear straight walk to the standing point and a clear walk out, the cooldown is over (30 s, `printCooldownSeconds`, saved by UUID, counted from the end of the print), a coin is there (the slot's stock, else a coin in the main hand that matches the slot's kind, else an identical coin in a Hub), and every worn stack fits in a free slot of the Hubs or the Locker. **Timeline:** entry walk at 0.2 blocks a tick, at least 12 ticks, the doors opening from 0 to 100 percent as the player comes; the 80-tick cycle of the README (doors shut by tick 16, the ring down and up, the arms, steam at tick 64); tick 40 of the cycle is the one transaction (take the coin, stow armor, off hand and hotbar into the Hubs nearest first then the Locker, put the kit on); the walk out at 0.2 blocks a tick to the cell 2 blocks in front; the doors close in 12 ticks; then the cooldown starts. A typical print (started 2.5 blocks out) takes 20 + 80 + 15 + 12 = 127 ticks, 6.35 s. **Held:** no input, no attacks, item use, block use, drops (a dropped item comes back) or damage (except damage that bypasses invulnerability: the void, /kill), no mount; the server puts the player back on the path when they are more than 0.75 blocks off. When the walk out ends they are let go standing still on the exit cell (since 1.1.1: put on it when more than 0.5 blocks off). **Stops before tick 40** (logout, death, dimension change, the station broken, the coin or the room gone at tick 40): nothing is used, the doors close, the player is let go in front (a logout saves them there). The print is not saved: a restarted server starts every station free.
* **Camera (client, `LoadoutCinematic`).** ENTRY behind and above the player, PRINT inside the cavity looking at the player (wide field of view, a white flash, steam and a hiss at tick 40), EXIT a pull-back that settles into the player's own eyes in the last 10 ticks. The player's model is drawn, the HUD and hand are hidden; Esc or a fresh press of sneak skips (the sneak still held from a quick print does not); it ends at once on logout, death, dimension change, the station broken or any error. Off with `loadoutCinematic` in `eternal-defense-client.toml`. With the shots on or off, the printed player's client draws no block outline from the walk in until the doors have shut, and no client draws one on a station whose print is playing (1.1.3). Other players see the walk, the doors, ring, arms, sparks and steam, never the camera.
* **Loadout Flare.** Sold at the Support Platform's Shop for **5 Ardent Energy**. Thrown like the Supply Flare (the capsule hits about 12 s after the shot outdoors, 7 s under cover, with Quantum Tunneling needed under 3 or more blocks), and in another dimension only with the Dimensional Link. It needs a Loadout Station in the base, the cannon, and a coin in the thrower's active slot (else an identical coin in a Hub); the coin is taken only when the cannon fires. Refused, or if the coin is gone when the cannon fires, the flare (and the coin) stay with the player and the chat says why. One Loadout call at a time per player.
* **Capsule.** A 1 x 1 x 2 pod (the owner's model). Since 1.1.1 there is no parachute: it appears 96 blocks above its cell (`CAPSULE_DROP_HEIGHT`) and plunges at 4 blocks a tick (24 ticks), passing through anything on the way, trailing smoke and flame, so it hits as the countdown ends. The impact: the game's explosion sound (volume 4) and an anvil clang, an explosion burst, smoke, the floor's block thrown up and a ring of cloud, and a view shake for players within 40 blocks (3.2 degrees at the spot, fading with distance, 26 ticks); it breaks no blocks and hurts no one. It stands up facing its owner on the flare's cell, or the nearest free two-block-high cell within 3 blocks, else the old supply parcel brings the kit; **its door opens by itself 8 ticks after the impact** (`CAPSULE_OPEN_DELAY`), with a hiss of steam. Only its owner opens its menu: the kit in its own slots (take only) and **Equip**, which puts each piece into its own slot only when that slot is empty (the rest stays in the capsule). Closing the menu with the kit still inside leaves the door open; emptied, the door shuts, steam blows from its flank vents and it folds away. Left unopened it lasts as long as a supply parcel (30 minutes), then spills its kit and folds away. The door's state travels in the block's update tag, so a player arriving later sees it as it is.
* **Sounds.** The radio answer to a Loadout call is one of the owner's four Loadout lines (`flare.loadout_chatter_1..4`, 4.6 to 7.6 s, never the same twice in a row; the no-voice build plays the supply call's game sound). The station's door and steam sounds are configurable placeholders: `doorSound` (`minecraft:block.piston.extend`) and `steamSound` (`minecraft:block.fire.extinguish`) in `[loadout]`.
* **Network.** No packet was added: the menu uses vanilla buttons and container data, the print and the capsule travel in block update tags. Protocol stays 41.

## 6. Economy and pacing targets

Goals the numbers are tuned against:
* A won raid by a reasonably efficient team replenishes ammunition and moves progress.
* Basic support (cannon 12, supply 3, return 2, fire 6) is affordable after the first two or three raids, before special raids appear.
* The first upgrades arrive in about one raid each (cannon 3 to 5 energy; beacon 4).
* A poor raid is recoverable through ordinary play (night mobs, farms, Exchange sell-offs; cheap repairs).
* Factory and target farming both work; **factory is clearly faster**.
* Rough income: standalone tier 3 raid is about 8 + drops of 80/12 + bonus; the whole cannon is about 25 average raids of standalone income. **(unverified: all numbers are design estimates; no long playtest yet.)**

Tuning knobs: `Economy`, `CannonUpgrades`, `SupportRules`, `RaidRewards`, `StandaloneBalance`, `ArdentEnergy`/`DefenseConfig`, `RaidTypes` (`CHANCE_PERCENT`, `FIRST_SPECIAL_RAID`, count factors), `RaidMarch` (`PROGRESS_BLOCKS`, `STUCK_SECONDS`, `GRACE_SECONDS`, `BREACH_BUSY_SECONDS`, `MAX_GATES`, `DRY_LIMIT`, `FIRING_DISTANCE`, `ENGAGED_RADIUS`, `LOOKAHEAD_BLOCKS`, `GATE_DROP_BLOCKS`, `GATE_FLUID_SPAN`, `DESTINATION_SPACING`, `CHANNEL_SECONDS`, `DAMAGE_DELAY_SECONDS`, `MAX_DELAY_SECONDS`, `TELEGRAPH_SECONDS`, `GATE_JOIN_RADIUS`, `GATE_CAPACITY`), `RaidSpawns` (`WEDGES`, `PROBE_STEP`, `PROBE_FROM`, `MAX_STEP`, `MAX_DROP`, `RESCUE_TRIES`, `RESCUE_PLAYER_DISTANCE`).

## 8. Technical notes for designers
* Network protocol **34** since 0.0.27 (28 from 0.0.11 to 0.0.19; 29 with Tactical Operations, 0.0.20; 30 when `MissionSync` gained the mission id, 0.0.21; 31 for the FOB removal confirmation and screen refresh, 0.0.23; 32 for the gate screens, 0.0.24; 33 for the SOS and repair, 0.0.25; 34 for the Tactical Enchanting Table's menu, 0.0.27); bump on any packet or menu-behaviour change. Message 14 carries raid marks, exclamation marks and parachutes; the kitchen preview is its own message; the v2 meal HUD message 15 was removed.
* Server owns all rules; clients render. Campaign state in `CampaignData`; per-player support state in `SupportData`; prepared meals in `PlayerMeals` SavedData, kitchen inventories/links and communal servings in block entities.
* Raid persistent flags are not synced automatically, so marks (red exclamation mark, red parachute) use their own idempotent packet sent every second. Meal MobEffects and backend attributes use native synchronization; v4 adds no packet.
* Operator test commands: `/eternaldefense test-raid <type>`, `/eternaldefense next-raid <type>`.
* Opt-in switches (system properties, all off by default): `-Deternaldefense.stuckLog=true` (gate diagnostics: every trigger, channel, interruption, teleport and withdrawal as one `[stuck]` line), `-Deternaldefense.stuckTests=true` (registers the raider-gate GameTests and `/stuck-raider-test`), `-Deternaldefense.optimizeTests=true` (registers the Optimize Path GameTests and `/optimize-path-test`, 0.0.15; they borrow the campaign of a world with no beacon, so use a throwaway world), `-Deternaldefense.fobTests=true` (registers the FOB GameTests and `/fob-test`, 0.0.23; throwaway worlds only: they clear the FOB records and build a village in the sky at x 30008). **Since 1.0.0** the GameTests, their switches and commands live in the `gametest` source set: development runs (`runServer`, `runGameTestServer`, the QA client) load them, the release jars do not; `stuckLog` stays in the release.
* V4 builds and standalone runtime checks passed: 70 beacon unit tests (7 guide tests remain Gradle up-to-date), 20 kitchen/gun/raid/effect server cases, optional LesRaisins radius/ownership and a real save/restart. Exact artifacts, client status and remaining playtests: `docs/MESS-HALL-V4-TESTING.md` and validation JSON.

## Fabrication, compatibility and recovery

**Other mods (1.1.8).** Elite powers are attribute modifiers, goals and events, so they work on any mob; the size works through Forge's size event. Another mod's boss (the `forge:bosses` tag or 150+ default health) is never sent at the beacon unless the raid spawned it (`BeaconCombat.bossOutsideRaid`). Every feature that calls another mod by reflection logs once and is skipped when that mod changed (`Compat.warn`): a missing raid cache item or LesRaisins supply is left out of the cache; a Cataclysm boss that cannot be woken is replaced by a ravager; without TaCZ Attributes' reload field, reloads keep TaCZ's own timing; without TaCZ's explosive-bullet field, Demolition skips explosive bullets; ammunition TaCZ cannot describe keeps the default price; a weapon type that cannot be read is "other"; JEI shows the output without its costs; a gun pack catalogue that cannot be split goes as TaCZ's own packet; a changed Special Forces mod sends no soldiers (the wave's usual mobs come instead). The mixin hooks into TaCZ, LesRaisins, ModernFix and the entity renderer may miss their target (`require=0`) and then only that bonus is lost.

Workshop Fabrication is the third beacon tab (Overview → Upgrades → Workshop → Raid break); Settings moves beside Guide in the header. Cards in three groups (tables, kitchen, support and trade) with material icons and counts; since 0.0.30 the list scrolls (mouse wheel or dragging its bar; rows 28 px, a fade at an edge with more beyond, a Make button only on a whole row) instead of squeezing its rows, so more installations fit; 24 today (1.1.0), the Loadout Station and the Logistics Center Hub the dearest. It sells the Weapon, Ammo, Attachment and Armor Platforms, Mess Hall Mk I, Cook Pot, Bowl Dispenser, Milk Dispenser, Support Platform, Support Cannon and Exchange Shop, and later additions up to the Logistics Center Hub (0.0.40) and the Loadout Station (1.1.0: 64 Reinforced Beacon Plating, 8 Restoration Matrices, 32 iron ingots, 16 glass). Prices are centralized in `Economy`, with atomic payment/output-space validation. The weapon table no longer exposes remote purchase/upgrade actions. Each of the four tables upgrades at its own interface and shows all four nearby Age badges; the current badge has a larger cyan frame. Upgrade hover costs show the material icon beside its name.

The Bowl Dispenser occupies **1 wide × 1 deep × 2 tall** inside the beacon zone. Right-click consumes one stored bowl; empty-both-hands crouch-right-click opens one inventory slot accepting at most 64 bowls. Upper-half interaction forwards to the anchor; breaking either half drops stock once. Stock persists in the block entity. There is no unlimited bowl creation.

The recovery shovel cancels attack damage before normal combat and applies intrinsic **Knockback II** (native strength 1.0 before target resistance). Might, Strength or other damage bonuses cannot turn it into a damaging weapon. Creative contents list starter installations by category, with one item named **Mess Hall**. Upgraded hall blocks remain accessible by upgrades/commands.

All Universal crafting paths reject TaCZ **FUEL** reload guns, including Apocalypse **bf1:ef46** and **bf1:wex**. These require Field Radio refills and use custom inspect/animation handling. The reported crash was not reproduced with the owner’s exact addon set; exclusion is the authorized fallback. Normal magazine/single-round reload weapons remain available. No generic catch around gun combat hides unrelated crashes.

## V6 kitchen additions

The Milk Dispenser uses Forge milk in an 8,000 mB tank with a fill bar and two inventory slots: filled containers and returned empties. Full buckets add 1,000 mB; Milk Bottles add 250 mB.
